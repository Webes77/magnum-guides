Magnum AI Weekly Field Note / No. 09

Check your spreadsheet before you send the numbers

What it is
A prompt that checks your spreadsheet for mistakes before you quote, invoice or pay from it.
What you get
A list of exactly which cells are wrong, so you only need to check a few rows yourself.
What you do
  1. Open the spreadsheet you're about to quote, invoice or pay from.
  2. Open a new chat, attach the spreadsheet and paste the prompt below.
  3. Check the rows it flags, then fix them yourself.
The prompt
You are a careful bookkeeper who checks spreadsheets for a living and gets paid for finding the mistake nobody else saw.

This spreadsheet is for [what the sheet is for]. The totals that matter most are [the totals that matter]. I am going to quote from it, pay from it or send it to someone, so a wrong number costs me real money.

Check every formula and every total. Work only from what is in the file. Do not change a single cell yet, because I want to look at each fix before it goes in. If you cannot tell whether something is wrong, say so instead of guessing, because an invented error sends me chasing a problem I do not have.

Be plain and direct. Name the cell, not the category.

List what you find by sheet and cell. Look for broken references, typed-in numbers where a formula should be, rows missing a formula, ranges that stop short, copied formulas that look wrong, and totals that do not add up to the rows above them.

For each problem give the cell, what is wrong, how sure you are (certain, or needs a human look) and the safest fix. Put the problems that change a total first. Then tell me which rows each key total actually counted, so I can check a few myself. If you find nothing wrong, say so in one line and stop.

Why it works

A wrong measurement still fits on the tape measure. You only find out once you've cut the timber and it doesn't fit. A spreadsheet total works the same way, it doesn't look wrong, it just looks like a number. This prompt gets a second set of eyes on every formula before you quote, invoice or pay, the same way you'd get someone to check the tape before the saw goes near the timber.
