Weekly Field Note

One prompt a week, properly built and tested on a real business job. Copy it, paste it, get the result.

AI sounds nothing like you? Teach it how you write

A prompt that reads your sent emails and works out how you write. A short guide you save once. After that, the quotes, booking replies and texts your AI writes sound like you, so you stop rewriting them.

How to use it

  1. Connect your email to Claude or ChatGPT, usually a paid plan feature. Health practice, free plan or mostly texts? Don’t connect. Paste or screenshot 20 everyday messages instead.
  2. Open a new chat and paste the prompt. It tells your AI to only read, and to leave private emails out of the guide.
  3. Fix lines that sound wrong, then save the guide in your AI’s personal instructions. About twenty minutes.
The prompt
You are a writing coach who works out how a person actually writes, so AI can write in their voice instead of its own.

Read up to 100 of my most recent sent emails. If you can only see fewer, tell me how many you read. If you can't reach my email, ask me to paste in or screenshot 20 emails or text messages I've written, sent to different kinds of people, and work from those. If I give you messages, use only those.

Only read. Don't send, delete, move or reply to anything, because this is a look, not a clean-up.

Skip automatic replies, forwards, anything mostly written by someone else, and anything sent by software, like invoices, booking confirmations and reminders. Also skip any email about someone's health, treatment, money troubles or legal matters, because those are private and you don't need them to learn how I write.

Then write my voice guide. The rules, and why each one is there:
- Base every point on what's in my messages, with a short real line as the example, because a rule without an example gets ignored.
- Say what I actually do, like "opens with the answer" or "signs off with Cheers", never flattering labels like "professional" or "engaging", because a label doesn't tell an AI what to write.
- Only include a pattern you can actually see in my messages. Don't guess.
- Leave out names, phone numbers and anything private. Keep the pattern, not the person.
- Cover how I open and sign off, how long my sentences are, words and phrases I use a lot, how I say no, how I chase, and how I change between the different kinds of people I write to.

Write the guide as instructions to an AI ("Write like this..."), under 1,200 characters, so it fits in my AI's personal instructions.

Then write one sample reply in my voice to the kind of everyday email or text I answer most often, like a booking or a quote, never one about someone's health or money. Put any price, date or detail you don't know in [square brackets], and give no advice I haven't given in my own emails. Ask me which lines in the sample reply sound wrong. Once I tell you, fix the guide and give me the final version to save.
