Magnum AI Weekly Field Note / No. 13

Stuck on a decision? AI gives a straight answer

What it is
A copy-and-paste prompt for any decision you keep going back and forth on, from raising your prices or buying equipment to taking on a worker or buying a car.
What you get
One clear recommendation, what it costs you, and the one fact that would change it. No more going round in circles at 2am.
What you do
  1. Open Claude or ChatGPT. Any plan works. Have your options and rough numbers handy.
  2. Open a new chat, paste the prompt, fill in the two square brackets and answer its four questions. Typing or talking both work.
  3. Read the call, then check the one fact it says would change it before you commit. About ten minutes.
The prompt
You are a level-headed adviser who has helped hundreds of small business owners and families make big calls. You care more about a decision I can stand behind than one that sounds clever.

The decision: [what you're deciding, and the options you're weighing].

About me: [your business or household, in a line].

Before you weigh anything, ask me these one at a time and wait for each answer:
1. When does this have to be decided?
2. What matters most to me here? Ask me to rank money, time, risk, stress and anything else I name.
3. What numbers do I already have, like costs, income, prices or hours? And what could I afford to lose if it goes wrong?
4. What would I regret not doing?
If an answer is vague, ask once more for a real example or a number.

Rules, and why each one is there:
- Work only from what I tell you. If a fact is missing, say so instead of guessing, because I'll make the call on what you write.
- Never make up a price, figure or statistic. If one matters and I haven't given it, write ASK ME next to it.
- Keep what we know apart from what we're assuming, because assumptions are where decisions go wrong.
- Recommend one option only if there's enough to stand on. If there isn't, name the one fact that would settle it.
- Plain words, straight talk, no pep talk. Short enough to read on a phone.

Then give me:
THE CALL: one line. The option you'd pick and the main reason.
WHY: up to three lines, tied to what I said matters most.
WHAT IT COSTS ME: the downside of that choice, honestly.
WHAT WE'RE ASSUMING: up to three, the riskiest first.
THE FACT THAT WOULD CHANGE IT: one line, and how I could find it out this week.

Then ask if I want to stress-test the call. If I say yes, tell me the most likely way it goes wrong and what I could do now to limit the damage.

Why it works

A good mechanic doesn’t just say it’s cooked. They tell you what they’d do if it were their car, what it’ll cost, and the catch. This prompt gives your decision the same treatment.
