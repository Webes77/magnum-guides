Magnum AI Weekly Field Note / No. 10

Taglines and subject lines that don't sound like everyone else's

What it is
A copy-and-paste prompt for a business name, tagline, headline or email subject line.
What you get
AI gives everyone the same first answer, competitors included. This gives you five, two of them lines nobody else will have.
What you do
  1. Decide what you need options for, a name, a tagline, a headline or a subject line, and who will see it.
  2. Open a new chat, fill in the brackets, and paste the prompt below.
  3. Pick the one that fits, or ask for five more if none of them do.
The prompt
You are a working copywriter for small businesses. You know the first answer an AI gives is the one everyone else gets, so you work to give me options a competitor would not land on.

I need five different [what you need: taglines, business names, email subject lines or headlines] for [what it is for].

The people who will see it: [who they are, and where they will see it]. What I want them to do or feel: [the one thing you want from them].

The rules, and why each one is there:
- Next to each option, give a number from 0 to 100 for how likely another AI is to give this exact answer. I use it to tell the safe options from the unusual ones, so be honest about it.
- Order the five from most likely to least likely.
- Make the last two options less than 10 per cent likely. They do not have to be good, only different. I will pick, or ask for more.
- Use only what I have told you about my business. If an option needs a fact I did not give you, leave it out, because an invented claim in my name costs me trust.
- Give me the line and the number only. An explanation makes a weak option sound stronger than it is.

Plain and direct, with no hype words. One line per option.

Give me the five lines, each followed by its number. Then stop.

Why it works

Get three quotes before a job, not one, because the first number is only the going rate. A name or a tagline works the same way: the first idea is the safe average everyone already has. Five options, each with a number on how common it is, show you what's ordinary and what isn't, so you choose with your eyes open.
