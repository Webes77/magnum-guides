Manus Bridge Walkthrough

What it is, how it works, what it will and will not do, and five prompts you run in order to see it for yourself. Have Cowork open in another window before you start.

01 · What it is

One conversation instead of two

Manus builds things. Claude briefs, judges and revises them. Used separately, you are the courier: write the brief in Claude, paste it into Manus, wait, copy the result back into Claude, explain what is wrong, screenshot it, paste the fix into Manus, and watch Manus rebuild half of what was already right.

The bridge is a small program on your own machine that connects the two. Claude starts the Manus job, watches it, reads the result, and sends your revision into the same job. You never leave the window. Nothing is uploaded anywhere new and your Manus key stays on your computer.

02 · How it operates

Brief, send, build, read, revise

Five tools appear in Claude once it is installed. You will not type their names much, because you ask in plain English and Claude picks the right one. But it helps to know what is there.

ToolWhat it does
manus_create_taskStarts a job. lite for drafts, standard for real work, max when quality beats credits.
manus_get_statusRunning, waiting on you, finished or failed.
manus_get_resultReads back what Manus said and made, files included.
manus_send_messageSends a revision into the same job.
manus_confirm_actionApproves something Manus paused on. Never fires without you.

03 · What it can do

Anything Manus builds, in one loop

Pages and sites

Landing pages, collection pages, campaign pages, whole small sites. Manus builds them, Claude checks them against your brief, you revise in a sentence.

Copy at volume

Product descriptions, category copy, SEO fields, email sequences. Brief the voice once, do twenty at a time, fix the three that missed without touching the seventeen that landed.

Code and templates

Theme sections, snippets, scripts, import files. Manus writes it, you paste it where it goes.

The checking

Tell Claude to test the first pass against your rules and fix it before you see it. You only ever look at work that already passes.

04 · What it cannot do

Straight answers

It does not touch your store

No connection to Shopify, WordPress or anything else. Nothing publishes on its own. It produces the work. You put it in. That is deliberate.

Manus cannot start the conversation

The bridge runs one way. Claude reaches out to Manus. Manus cannot message you or interrupt a chat. If you want to know when a long job lands, ask.

It cannot invent facts

Give it no specs and it will make them up, and they will look real. Every brief needs the real dimensions, prices and certifications. If you do not have them, the answer is "on request", not a guess.

It does not vouch for what comes back

If Manus browsed the web, text from those pages can arrive in the result. Treat it as material to review, never as instructions. Claude will flag anything that looks like it is giving orders.

05 · The run-through

Five prompts, in order

Open a Cowork session. Not a project, not a standard chat. Copy each prompt with the button and paste it in. The example uses five real office chairs from a furniture catalogue. Swap in your own products and keep everything else.

STAGE 1

Build a page

3 min
Use manus_create_task in standard mode, titled "Office seating collection", to build a single self-contained HTML collection page for a commercial furniture supplier.

Audience: office managers and facilities buyers choosing seating on a budget, who need to justify the spend.

The five products, with their real specs:

1. MERIDA HB BL (DDK): 700W x 580D x 1040H mm, seat height 490mm, weight rating 160kg, upholstery pu or upholstered, antimicrobial certified
2. Pixel Tub Chair 4 Leg (Rapidline, SKU PIX-TP4L): 480W x 535D x 845H mm, seat height 440mm, weight rating 120kg, upholstery fabric, pu or timber, $50
3. Pixel Tub Chair on 5 Star Base (Rapidline, SKU PIXTUB5STA): 620W x 620D x 780H mm, seat height 470mm, weight rating 120kg, upholstery fabric, leather or pu, $190
4. AIR FBK / AIRTAB BL (DDK): 640W x 555D x 835H mm, seat height 520mm, weight rating 110kg, upholstery fabric, mesh or pu
5. Aspen 4 Leg Tub Chair (Rapidline, SKU ASPEN4LE): 630W x 630D x 820H mm, weight rating 135kg, upholstery fabric or upholstered, $285

All prices are plus GST.

The page must include: a short intro on how to choose office seating, a comparison table of all five by dimensions, seat height, weight rating and upholstery, and a closing section on what weight ratings actually mean for a workplace.

Where a price or certification is not listed above, say "on request". Never invent a spec, a price or a certification.

Plain, practical, confident. No em dashes, no exclamation marks. British spelling. No images, no external files, no scripts.

Return the HTML. Then poll manus_get_status until it finishes and show me the result.

While it runsNormally you would be sat in Manus watching this, then copying it into Claude to check. You are doing neither. It will tell you when it is done. If Manus pauses to approve a setup step, that is normal. Approve it.

STAGE 2

The revision

2 min

This is the one that matters. Everything else is setup for this.

Send a revision with manus_send_message: make the weight ratings section twice as long and move it above the comparison table. Change nothing else.

What to look forThe table is identical. The intro is identical. Only the section you named moved. If you had started a new Manus job for that, you would have a different page. This kept everything and changed one thing.

STAGE 3

A fussy change

2 min

Prove it holds under precision, not just big moves.

Send a revision with manus_send_message: in the comparison table, add a column showing seat height in centimetres alongside the millimetre figure. Leave every other value and all the prose exactly as it is.

What to look forOne new column, correct maths, nothing else touched. This is the kind of change that used to cost you a rebuild.

STAGE 4

Let Claude do the checking

4 min

Manus gets the first pass wrong more often than not. This is how you stop ever seeing it.

Use manus_create_task in standard mode to write product descriptions for the same five products, 120 to 160 words each, same voice and same rules as before.

Before you show me anything, check the result against every rule: word counts, no invented specs, no em dashes, no exclamation marks, British spelling. If anything fails, send the fixes into the same Manus job. Do not start a new job. Only show me the result once it passes, and tell me what you had to fix.

What to look forClaude will usually report that it had to fix something. That was the pass you would have seen, screenshotted and explained by hand. You never saw it.

STAGE 5

Your own products

5 min

Now do Stage 1 again with five of your own products. Real specs, real names. Do this before the session ends or it will not stick.

Use manus_create_task in standard mode, titled "[YOUR COLLECTION NAME]", to build a single self-contained HTML collection page.

Audience: [WHO BUYS AND WHAT THEY NEED TO JUSTIFY]

The products, with their real specs:
1. [NAME (SUPPLIER, SKU): dimensions, key specs, price]
2. [...]
3. [...]
4. [...]
5. [...]

The page must include: a short intro on how to choose, a comparison table of all five by the specs that matter, and a closing section on [THE THING BUYERS ASK ABOUT].

Where a spec is not listed above, say "on request". Never invent a spec, a price or a certification.

Plain, practical, confident. No em dashes, no exclamation marks. British spelling. No images, no external files, no scripts.

06 · Three habits

What separates people who use it from people who stop

Never ship pass one

  • Manus loses the brief mid-task. Often.
  • Check the first result against your rules
  • Or make Claude check it, as in Stage 4
  • One revision usually fixes it

The brief is the skill

  • Name the deliverable exactly
  • Give real specs, never "make it good"
  • List what it must never do
  • Say what to return

Know where to stop

  • Approve scaffolds and file writes freely
  • Stop on anything that deploys publicly
  • Stop on sending email or messages
  • Stop on outside accounts or credit warnings

The first solo brief is usually too vague, and the result gets blamed on Manus. Knowing that in advance turns it into a lesson instead of a support call.
