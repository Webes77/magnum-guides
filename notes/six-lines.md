# The 6 Levers as six lines

The one wording of the six levers. Every place that teaches them uses these
lines exactly: the AI Foundations deck, the Prompt Shelf primer, the
`six-lines` shelf card, the six-lever-prompt-builder skill, and James's own
talk track. Change a line here first, then everywhere else in the same pass.

The labels and their order are hard rule 3 and never change. Only the line
under each one is teaching copy.

| Lever | The line | The one thing to say about it |
|---|---|---|
| Role | You are a ___ who works with ___. | A real job and its field. Nothing more. |
| Context | Here is the situation: ___. | What it cannot see. What it does not know, it invents. |
| Constraints | Stick to these rules: ___, because ___. | A rule with its reason gets kept. |
| Tone | Sound like ___ (a person, not a word). | Someone you can picture. "Friendly" gets you exclamation marks. |
| Format | Lay it out as ___. | The shape you want back. One example beats a description. |
| Output | Give me ___. Then stop. | The finished thing, and where it ends. |

How it is used, in two sentences. Write the six lines top to bottom and fill
in the blanks; a line with nothing to say is left out. When an answer comes
back weak, look for the thin line and fix that one.

Why it replaced the old teaching, 25 Sep. Each lever had been explained three
different ways (a question on the overview slide, a rule on its own slide, a
third line on the shelf), so a learner met eighteen things for six ideas. The
deck also said "a diagnostic, not a formula" two slides before "that order,
every time". And Format and Output read as the same question to a beginner.
One sentence starter per lever fixes all three: it is the formula and the
diagnostic at once, and "Then stop" is what keeps Output apart from Format.

## Paste-ready block for skills

Paste this over the lever descriptions in the six-lever-prompt-builder skill,
and into any other skill that explains the levers.

```
THE 6 LEVERS, AS SIX LINES
Role, Context, Constraints, Tone, Format, Output. That order. Never renamed.
Teach and build every prompt as six fill-in lines:
Role: You are a ___ who works with ___.
Context: Here is the situation: ___.
Constraints: Stick to these rules: ___, because ___.
Tone: Sound like ___ (a person, not a word).
Format: Lay it out as ___.
Output: Give me ___. Then stop.
A line with nothing to say is left out. A weak answer means one line was
thin: find it and fix that line.
```

## Filled in: the session one demo

This is P4 in AI Foundations and `overdue-invoice` on the shelf, word for word.

```
You are a bookkeeper who works with small businesses.

Here is the situation: a client we want to keep is 30 days late paying an
invoice. This has never happened before. I sent one polite reminder two
weeks ago and had no reply.

Stick to these rules: under 120 words, because it has to be read in one
go. No threat and no apology, because either one makes it harder for them
to pay. Make it easy for them to pay, and easy to tell me if something is
wrong.

Sound like an owner who is good at the job and does not need to chase.
Warm, level, straight.

Lay it out as a subject line, then the email as plain text I can paste.

Give me the email. Then stop.
```
