# 07 · Industry rules for social posts: pub and adventure tourism

Researched 24 Sep 2026. Every page fetch was blocked from the sandbox, so every claim below is read from search snippets, not the full page. Grades: A is the regulator or code body's own page, B is a law firm or reputable press, C is a blog or vendor. Not legal advice; a client with a live question goes to their own lawyer or the regulator.

## 1. Pub and bar

### ABAC Responsible Alcohol Marketing Code

- Who it binds. Formally binding on signatories and members of the major industry associations, but the ABAC panel hears complaints about any alcohol marketing, signatory or not, and a non-signatory licensee who ignores a decision can be referred to the state liquor licensing authority. So a pub should treat it as applying. (A: abac.org.au/about/faq/ ; A: abac.org.au/about/adjudication-panel/ ; B: tglaw.com.au insight "Is the ABAC Code really voluntary")
- Complaints go through Ad Standards and anyone can lodge one. (A: abac.org.au/about/lodging-complaints/)
- No link to success. Marketing must not show alcohol as causing or contributing to personal, business, social, sporting, sexual or other success, and must not suggest a social occasion depends on alcohol. (A: abac.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ABAC-Responsible-Alcohol-Marketing-Code-21-5-25.pdf)
- No drinking before or during activity needing alertness or coordination: driving, boats, machinery, swimming. (A: same Code PDF)
- Minors. A minor is under 18. Code also bars strong appeal to minors (content, characters, people who look under 25 are the usual test in the Code; confirm wording against the current PDF). (A: same Code PDF, snippet; the under-25 detail is from training knowledge and is marked unverified)
- Placement on social. Alcohol marketing may sit on age-restricted platforms that require login with full date of birth and can hide alcohol content from unregistered adults; where a platform cannot exclude minors, the audience must reasonably be at least 80% adults. (A: Code PDF, 2025)
- User comments and reshares count. User-generated content on a page the advertiser controls, including UGC it shares or endorses, is in scope; moderate it, ideally before it posts, at least regularly after. (A: abac.org.au Best Practice for Responsible Digital Marketing, 4 Jun 2025, abac.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Best-Practice-for-Responsible-Digital-Marketing-4-6-25.pdf)

### Queensland Liquor Act and OLGR

- Section 142ZZC: a licensee must not advertise free liquor, discounted liquor or the sale price of liquor for consumption on premises (restaurants excepted). Happy hours, all you can drink and similar may only be advertised inside the venue, not visible or audible outside. A social post is outside the venue. (A: business.qld.gov.au/industries/hospitality-tourism-sport/liquor-gaming/liquor/compliance/advertising-promotions ; B: Mullins Lawyers, HopgoodGanim)
- Guideline 60 lists unacceptable promotions: emotive names like "thirsty Thursday", "beat the clock", "drink till you drop"; free or discounted drinks that encourage rapid consumption. (A: publications.qld.gov.au Liquor guideline 60 ; A: business.qld.gov.au illegal-unacceptable promotions page)
- Free or discounted drink promotions need a documented management plan with harm minimisation. (A: Guideline 60, via business.qld.gov.au ; C: liquorlicencequeensland.com.au)
- Guideline 12 covers packages that bundle a meal or accommodation with alcohol. (A: business.qld.gov.au liquor-guidelines/advertising-packages)

Practical reading: a pub can post the food, the band, the room and the atmosphere freely. Posting a drink price or a drink discount on Instagram is the thing most likely to breach the Act. Treat "$8 pints" as an in-venue sign, not a post, unless the licensee's lawyer says the offer is lawful to advertise.

### Meta and Instagram

- Alcohol ads in Australia must target 18 and over at minimum. (A: transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/alcohol/)
- Branded content and partnership ads with alcohol must use audience restrictions (age, location); the paid partnership tag applies them. (C: hawtads.com, simplysocialmedianm.com; direction matches Meta's own page)
- Page-level age restriction for alcohol accounts exists in Facebook and Instagram settings; research found many Australian alcohol brands did not switch it on. (B: Public Health Research and Practice, connectsci.au, vol 32 issue 2). The exact menu path was not verified; name no button.

### Pub: before you post

1. No drink prices, discounts, free drinks or happy hour times in any post. Those stay on signs inside the venue.
2. No "thirsty Thursday" style names or anything that dares people to drink fast or drink more.
3. Nobody in the picture who is, or looks, under 25, and nothing that appeals to kids (cartoons, toys, school themes).
4. Drinks never shown as the reason the night, the date, the win or the deal went well.
5. No drinking near driving, boats, pools, the beach water or machinery. Not even implied.
6. Turn on the age restriction (18+) on the Facebook page and Instagram account, and on every boosted post.
7. Read comments and tagged reshares weekly; hide anything that breaks these rules, because shared posts count as yours.
8. Sell the venue, not the drink: food, music, sport on the screens, the view, the staff.

Must always include/check: "No price or discount on alcohol, nobody under 25 on screen, and the account is set to 18+."

## 2. Adventure tourism

### ACCC and the Australian Consumer Law

- Misleading claims. It is illegal to make false or misleading representations; this is the general ACL rule and covers safety and suitability claims such as "100% safe" or "suitable for everyone". No ACCC page specific to adventure tourism safety claims was found. (A: accc.gov.au/consumers/advertising-and-promotions/false-or-misleading-claims)
- Reviews and testimonials. A business must not create fake reviews or arrange others to; must not cherry-pick only good reviews or suppress bad ones; incentivised reviews must be disclosed; outsourcing does not move the liability. Meriton was penalised $3 million over TripAdvisor review suppression. (A: accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/online-reviews-for-product-and-services ; B: Addisons, Lexology, LegalVision)
- Waivers. Section 139A of the Competition and Consumer Act lets recreational service providers exclude consumer guarantees for death and personal injury only, not for reckless conduct causing significant injury, and a supplier that implies customers have no rights risks misleading them. (A: austlii.edu.au CCA s139A ; B: Coulter Legal, Harwood Andrews May 2026, RMB Lawyers). Practical upshot: waiver wording belongs in the booking terms, not in a post, and a post must never say or imply "you sign away your rights".

### Queensland specifics

- No Queensland marketing rule specific to adventure operators was found. The Australian Adventure Activity Standard is a voluntary national good-practice framework; it is often cited as evidence an operator met legal duties. (A: outdoorsqueensland.com.au/industry/australian-adventure-activity-standard/ ; A: statements.qld.gov.au/statements/89528). Permits for activities in parks and marine areas are separate and not marketing rules (A: business.qld.gov.au tourism licences page).

### Customer photos

- A photo of an identifiable person is personal information under the Privacy Act. Consent is not always required, but the person must be made aware how the image will be used, and use for an unexpected purpose probably needs consent. (A: oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/handling-personal-information/posting-photos-and-videos). Note small businesses under $3 million turnover are often exempt from the Privacy Act, but the practical rule (ask first) stands because it is also the reputational rule. (Exemption from training knowledge, not verified this run.) Children: always get a parent's written yes.

### Drones

- Flying for business, including filming your own promo, needs the drone registered with CASA and at least RPA operator accreditation, inside the standard operating conditions (sub-2 kg excluded category; no RePL or ReOC needed if within them). Larger drones or flying outside the conditions needs a licensed operator. (A: casa.gov.au/drones/operator-accreditation-certificate/drone-weight-categories-and-requirements ; A: casa.gov.au get-or-renew-your-operator-accreditation ; C: aaa.edu.au blog). Standard conditions include staying 30 m from people and not over crowds (training knowledge, confirm on CASA page). Simplest path: hire a licensed drone operator and keep their paperwork.

### Adventure: before you post

1. Never say "100% safe", "no risk", "totally safe" or "suitable for everyone". Say what you do: "qualified guides, full briefing, all gear supplied".
2. State real limits where they apply: minimum age, weight, fitness, swimming ability.
3. Show the safety gear being worn in every action shot. If a guest is not wearing it, do not post it.
4. Only real reviews, word for word, with the person's first name. Never post only the five stars and hide the rest, and say if a review earned a discount.
5. Ask before you post a customer's face, and get a parent's yes for any child. A line on the booking form covers most of it.
6. No waiver or legal wording in posts. It lives in the booking terms.
7. Drone footage only from a registered drone flown by an accredited operator, never over crowds or guests without them knowing.
8. Do not promise outcomes you cannot control: whales, waves, weather, "you will see".

Must always include/check: "No 'totally safe' or 'anyone can do it', safety gear visible, and a yes from every person you can recognise."
