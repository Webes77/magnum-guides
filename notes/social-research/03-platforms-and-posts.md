# 03 · Platforms, post types and cadence for a small Australian business that wants presence, not fame

Researched 23 September 2026. Every fact below comes from a web search run on that day.

**Method caveat, read first.** WebFetch was blocked by the sandbox egress proxy for every domain tried (datareportal.com, buffer.com, about.fb.com, services.google.com, tubefilter.com, constructionadvisor.com.au, jameshardie.com.au). So every claim here comes from **search result snippets**, not full pages I read. Where a snippet quotes a primary source (Meta newsroom, Google Help, eSafety), I grade it A for the source but the wording is the search engine's summary. Spot-check any figure that goes on a slide.

Grades: **A** = the platform's own current page or an official report. **B** = reputable press or research firm (DataReportal/We Are Social/Meltwater, Buffer, Metricool, Sprout, Hootsuite, BrightLocal, Roy Morgan). **C** = blog, agency or forum.

**A general point about the evidence.** The frequency studies (Buffer, Metricool, Hootsuite, Sprout) measure *growth and engagement* for accounts trying to grow. Our clients mostly want to *look alive when checked*. That goal needs a much lower bar, and none of these studies measure it directly. The "minimum credible cadence" below is therefore a judgement built from the evidence, not a measured number. Say so in the course.

### The under-16 ban (in force 10 December 2025)

Facebook, Instagram, Threads, TikTok, Snapchat, YouTube, X, Reddit, Twitch and Kick must take reasonable steps to stop Australians under 16 holding accounts. Parents and anyone 16+ are unaffected. Under-16s can still see public content that needs no login. WhatsApp and Messenger are not covered as of 10 December 2025. Source: eSafety Commissioner, https://www.esafety.gov.au/communities/sport/social-media-age-restrictions and https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions/faqs (A).

**This matters for the sports client.** eSafety has a page aimed at sport organisations. Clubs that relied on under-16 members following them for schedules and results need another channel. eSafety names WhatsApp and Messenger as unaffected, subject to child-safety policy. Source: https://www.esafety.gov.au/communities/sport/social-media-age-restrictions (A). So for a junior-heavy sports business, Facebook and Instagram talk to **parents**, and the kids are reached by messaging or email.

DataReportal-based commentary expects Instagram, TikTok and Snapchat headline numbers to fall 5% to 15% through 2026 from the ban, with Facebook largely unaffected. Source: search summary of https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-australia (B, but a forecast, not a measurement).

## 2. What each network is for, for a local business

This is judgement built on the evidence above.

| Network | What it is for, locally | Beginner verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Business Profile** | The listing that appears when someone searches your name or "builder near me". Reviews, photos, hours, posts. | **Everyone. Do this first.** See section 3. |
| **Facebook** | The biggest audience over 35 in Australia; local community groups where people ask "anyone know a good builder?"; reviews; Messenger enquiries. | **Everyone.** The default "are they real?" check for homeowners and parents. |
| **Instagram** | The visual portfolio. People who find you tap through your grid to judge the work. | **Yes for construction, retail, sport.** One Meta Business Suite post can go to both FB and IG. |
| **LinkedIn** | Commercial buyers, developers, architects, hiring. | **Construction only if they do commercial or B2B work**, and the owner's personal profile beats the company page. Skip for retail. |
| **YouTube** | Search engine for "how to". Long-form walkthroughs, project tours. | **Skip at first.** Upload the odd project tour later; Shorts can be the same vertical video as Reels. |
| **TikTok** | Discovery among younger adults; most time spent per day. | **Skip unless the owner enjoys video.** Useful for sport and some retail. Not needed to look credible. |
| **Threads** | Text conversation in the Meta family. 1.35M Australian users. | **Skip.** |
| **X** | News and politics; shrinking in Australia. | **Skip.** |
| **Pinterest** | Idea planning: renovations, interiors, fashion, gifting. | **Skip unless** retail in homewares, fashion, gifts, or a builder selling design. |
| **Nextdoor** | Operates in Australia (https://au.nextdoor.com/, A) but I found no Australian user numbers. | **Skip.** Local Facebook groups do this job in Australia (C, see section 6). |

Supporting evidence for Facebook's local role: "Facebook remains the highest-ROI social platform for most Australian trades businesses, particularly for reaching homeowners aged 35 to 65", and homeowners post in local community groups asking for tradie recommendations. Source: https://www.20minutemarketing.com.au/blog/digital-marketing-for-tradies-australia-2026 (C, agency).

## 3. Google Business Profile as a "social" channel

**Why it may matter more than Instagram for trades.** It is where the checking happens. Someone searching the business's name or the service in their suburb sees the profile, the star rating and photos before any social account.

- Google's own marketing: customers are **2.7x more likely to consider a business reputable** with a complete profile on Search and Maps, **70% more likely to visit** and **50% more likely to consider purchasing**. Source: Google, widely quoted, surfaced via https://www.shopify.com/retail/google-business-profile and https://www.socialpilot.co/reviews/blogs/google-business-profile-marketing (Google's claim, A in origin; I read it only through third parties, so C as seen). These are old Google figures, repeated for years; use as "Google says", not as fresh research.
- Photos: adding photos increases direction requests by 42% on average. Source: https://wisernotify.com/blog/google-business-profile-statistics/ (C; origin attributed to Google, date unclear).
- 97% of consumers read online reviews when evaluating local businesses; 41% "always" do (up from 29%); ~81% read reviews on Google. Source: BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/ (B, US panel).

**Posts on the profile.** Three types: Update, Offer, Event. Photos JPG or PNG, 10 KB to 5 MB. Update posts older than six months are archived unless they carry a date range; Offers and Events stay live for their set dates. Source: Google Business Profile Help, https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169 (A, via snippet).

Posts are best treated as **conversion assets, not ranking levers**: weekly posts lift click-through in the local panel but do not directly move map-pack position. Source: https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/google-business-profile-guide-every-feature-2026 (C). Google publishes a 2026 best-practices playbook (https://services.google.com/fh/files/helpcenter/services_gbp_best_practices_playbook_2026.pdf, A) which I could not open.

**Reviews.** Google's help pages cover getting and managing reviews: https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122 and https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050 (A). Guidance surfaced: reply personally, name the reviewer, keep replies short, reply promptly, and the profile must be verified to reply. Replies are moderated and can take from minutes up to 30 days to appear. Source: Google Business Profile Community guide, https://support.google.com/business/community-guide/318603570/ (A/C: Google-hosted community content).

**The practical point for the course:** a construction company with a complete profile, 30 photos of real jobs, replies to every review and one post a fortnight looks more credible to a searching homeowner than one with a busy Instagram and an empty Google listing.

## 4. Post-type taxonomy

### Content types (what the post is about)

| # | Type | What it is | Construction | Retail | Sport |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Proof of work** | Finished job, product in use, result | Core. Completed project, one line on the brief | New stock in store | Results, a class in full swing |
| 2 | **Before / after** | Two states, same angle | Core. The strongest single post for trades | Shop fit-out, styling | Member progress (with consent) |
| 3 | **Work in progress** | Site progress, stage reached | Core. Slab, frame, lock-up, handover | Unboxing a delivery | Training session, venue prep |
| 4 | **Behind the scenes** | How it gets done | Crew at smoko, the ute, tools | Packing orders, the back room | Coach setting up, equipment |
| 5 | **Team / people** | Who you would deal with | Strong. Face of the owner and crew builds trust | Staff picks | Coaches, staff |
| 6 | **Customer story / testimonial** | Review screenshot, quote, handover photo | Strong | Strong | Strong |
| 7 | **Useful tip / FAQ** | Answers a real customer question | "What does a site start actually involve?" | "How to choose..." | Technique tip, what to bring |
| 8 | **Community / local** | Local event, sponsorship, charity | Local footy sponsorship | Street event, market | Core. Club days, junior comps |
| 9 | **Offer / promotion** | Sale, booking window, new service | Rare. Maybe "booking Q1 builds now" | Core, but not every post | Term enrolments, trial class |
| 10 | **Safety / standards** | How you work safely and properly | Specific to construction; credibility for commercial buyers | n/a | Child safety, qualifications |

Evidence that "real people" content is what audiences want: 2025 Sprout Social Index, consumers most want to see front-line employees (48%), social teams (42%) and real customers (42%); authenticity and relatability are the traits valued most; about half say original content makes a brand stand out; a third find trend-jumping by brands embarrassing. Sources: https://sproutsocial.com/insights/index/ and https://investors.sproutsocial.com/news/news-details/2025/The-Days-of-Trend-Chasing-Are-Over-New-Research-from-Sprout-Social-Reveals-a-Third-of-Consumers-Think-Jumping-on-Viral-Trends-is-Embarrassing-for-Brands/ (B, global survey).

### Format types (how it is delivered)

| Format | Best for | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Single image** | Proof, quick updates, GBP posts | Lowest algorithmic priority on Instagram Explore (C) |
| **Carousel** (up to multiple images) | Before/after, a job in stages, a tip in steps | Metricool 2026: carousels earn 9x more saves than other formats; 0.50% engagement by followers vs 0.48% for Reels in Q2 2026 (B). Buffer 2026: on Instagram carousels earn 12% more engagement; on LinkedIn carousels are the top format by a wide margin (B) |
| **Reel / short vertical video** | Walkthroughs, time-lapse, reaching non-followers | Metricool 2026: Reels get 4x+ the interactions of a single image (B). Buffer 2026: Reels get 36% more reach than carousels (B) |
| **Story** (24 hours) | Low-effort daily "we're open, we're busy" | Hootsuite cites Mosseri suggesting two Stories a day (C, via Hootsuite) |
| **Text post** | Facebook updates, LinkedIn | Buffer 2026: on Facebook, image, video and text all land within one percentage point of each other on engagement (B) |

Sources: Metricool 2026 Instagram study, 24M posts from 375,118 accounts, https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/ and https://metricool.com/instagram-trends/ (B). Buffer State of Social Media Engagement 2026, 52M+ posts across 10 platforms, https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/ and https://buffer.com/resources/data-best-content-format-social-media/ (B). Instagram format priority: https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers (C).

**Plain summary for beginners:** carousels speak to people who already follow you; Reels reach people who do not. For "look credible when checked", carousels and photos of real work are enough. Reels are the upgrade, not the requirement.

## 5. Posting frequency and what the algorithms reward

### What the studies say (evidence, grade B)

- **Buffer, Instagram, 2M posts from 100k accounts:** 3 to 5 posts a week more than doubles follower growth versus fewer; 6 to 9 a week up to 3.7x; 10+ up to 5.5x. Sources: https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/ , reported at https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/study-shows-posting-more-instagram-leads-to-more-reach/757633/ (B).
- **Buffer, consistency, 4.8M channel-weeks:** accounts that did not post in a given week underperformed their own baseline; any posting beat none. Same source (B). **This is the finding that matters for our clients: consistency beats volume, and a skipped week costs you.**
- **Hootsuite 2026 guidance:** Instagram 3 to 5 a week (minimum three); Facebook 1 to 2 a day; LinkedIn far higher for Hootsuite itself. Hootsuite also says the ideal is unique to each brand and a manageable schedule beats burnout. Source: https://blog.hootsuite.com/how-often-to-post-on-social-media/ and https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-posting-schedule/ (B, but a vendor that sells scheduling).
- **Sprout / aggregated 2026 guidance:** LinkedIn 2 to 3 a week; Facebook 3 to 5 a week. Source: https://sproutsocial.com/insights/how-often-to-post-on-social-media/ and https://www.heyorca.com/blog/social-media-posting-frequency-by-platform-2026 (B / C).

**Separate evidence from marketing.** Every one of these companies sells scheduling software, and every study measures growth for accounts trying to grow. "Post more, grow more" is true in their data and irrelevant to a builder who wants a credible grid. No study I found measures "how stale can a page be before a customer doubts you". The Buffer consistency finding is the closest honest evidence.

### What the algorithms currently reward (2025 to 2026)

**Originality, on both Meta platforms. This is the most important change for anyone using AI.**

- **Facebook, July 2025:** Meta announced it would reduce distribution of duplicate and unoriginal content (reposting others' work without adding anything) and could remove monetisation for repeat offenders; about 500,000 accounts actioned in H1 2025. Sources: https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-cracking-down-unoriginal-content-ai-replicas/753001/ and https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/16/facebook-to-fight-unoriginal-content/ (B, reporting a Meta blog of 14 July 2025).
- **Facebook, 13 March 2026, "Rewarding Original Creators on Facebook":** content filmed or produced directly by the creator or Page owner counts as original; reused material only qualifies with something genuinely new added. Views and time spent on original Reels roughly doubled in H2 2025 versus H2 2024. Source: https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/ (A, via snippet).
- **Instagram, April 2026:** Mosseri: "If most of what you post to Instagram is someone else's content, your account is no longer going to be recommendable." Rolling 30-day window; aggregator accounts lose recommendations in Explore, Feed and Reels for non-followers; now covers photos and carousels, not only Reels. Sources: https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/ and https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator/ (B). The widely repeated "10 reposts in 30 days" and "70% visual similarity" thresholds appear only on blogs (C); do not teach them as fact.
- **Instagram ranking signals:** watch time, sends per reach (DM shares) and likes per reach, per Mosseri. Source: https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers (C reporting Mosseri). Small accounts: Mosseri said in a February 2026 AMA that accounts under 10,000 followers get tested with non-followers readily (C, same family of sources).
- **Instagram tone:** Mosseri's end-of-2025 memo said the polished aesthetic is over and Instagram will favour raw, real human content over AI-generated material in 2026. Source: https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers and similar (C). Consistent with the Meta originality posts above (A).
- **Facebook feed:** organic Page reach has declined for years and the feed now leans heavily on recommended content from accounts you do not follow; comments and shares count for more than likes. Source: https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/facebooks-2026-rules-for-reach-relevance/ (B/C). The "40%+ recommended content" figure is from a blog (C).

**The teaching point:** AI is safe to use for captions, planning and editing *your own* photos and video. Using AI to generate the pictures, or reposting other people's content, works against you on both Meta platforms. Real site photos taken on a phone are exactly what the algorithm and the customer both want. That is good news for a beginner course.

### Recommended minimum credible cadence (judgement, built from the above)

- **Google Business Profile:** 1 post a fortnight (update posts archive after six months, so a gap of months shows); new photos monthly; reply to every review within a few days.
- **Facebook + Instagram together** (cross-posted via Meta Business Suite): **2 posts a week** is the credible minimum; 3 is comfortable. Never go a full week silent (Buffer consistency finding). The C-grade builder guidance of "at least twice a week" agrees: https://www.elitepropertymarketing.com.au/instagram-marketing-for-builders/ (C).
- **Stories:** optional, 2 to 3 a week of whatever happened on site or in store. Zero pressure.
- **LinkedIn** (commercial construction only): 1 post a week from the owner's personal profile.

The acid test for "presence": **when a customer checks you, the newest post is under two weeks old, the grid shows real work, and every recent review has a reply.**

## 6. Construction and trades: what good looks like

**Content that works** (C-grade industry guides, consistent with the A/B originality evidence):
- Site progress in stages (slab, frame, lock-up, fit-out, handover), ideally the same angle each time so it becomes a before/after carousel at the end. https://www.hunterlabourhire.com.au/5-reasons-instagram-essential-construction-businesses/ , https://www.tradifyhq.com/blog/social-media-ideas-for-builders-and-construction-workers (C)
- Completed projects as a portfolio, the grid working as a gallery. same (C)
- Team and owner faces; crew on site. https://www.dreamcube.com.au/instagram-video-post-ideas-for-home-builders/ (C)
- Safety and standards: a clean, signed, fenced site is itself a credibility post for commercial buyers (judgement).
- Client handover photos and review screenshots.

**Australian examples found:**
- **Futureflip** (design and build, founder Neil Hipwell): over 80% of new business leads engaged with its Instagram profile before getting in touch. Source: James Hardie blog, https://www.jameshardie.com.au/blog/tips-to-build-your-business-part-1-social-media-for-builders (C, supplier blog, but the only hard Australian builder figure found). This is the best single stat for the construction client: people check the profile before they call.
- **Beth Builds** (Melbourne, carpenter Beth Moresi and family) and tradie accounts such as a building inspector exposing poor work on TikTok, and the lawn-mowing "Free Mow Fridays" transformation videos. Source: https://www.tradifyhq.com/blog/tradie-influencers-in-australia and https://www.trustedtradienetwork.com/blog/how-tradie-influencers-are-changing-construction (C). These are influencer-scale and **not** the model for our client, who only needs credibility. Useful to show the ceiling, not the target.

I did not verify any account handle or check that these accounts are current, because fetches were blocked. Check before naming any of them in a slide.

**Watch-outs for construction** (judgement): get client consent before showing a home or address; do not show number plates or house numbers; show PPE worn correctly, because a photo of a crew without it is a liability, not a post.

## 7. Minimum viable presence per client type

### Common setup checklist (all three)
1. Google Business Profile claimed and verified; category, service area or address, hours, phone, website, description; 10+ real photos; first review requests sent.
2. Facebook Page with the same name, logo, phone, hours, and a cover photo of real work; Messenger on.
3. Instagram professional account linked to the Page; bio saying what, where and how to contact; 9 real posts before promoting it (so the grid is not empty when checked).
4. Same name, logo and phone everywhere.
5. Meta Business Suite set up so one post goes to both.
6. A phone folder or album where every job, delivery or session drops a photo.

### Construction (credible, not famous)
- **Networks:** Google Business Profile (first), Facebook, Instagram. LinkedIn only if commercial.
- **Weekly rhythm (about 30 minutes):** 1 progress or completed-job post (carousel), 1 team/behind-the-scenes or tip post; reply to reviews; ask every finished client for a Google review. GBP post every second week, reusing the week's best job photo.

### Retail with no presence
- **Networks:** Google Business Profile (first, because hours and location are what people search), Instagram, Facebook. Pinterest only for homewares, fashion or gifts.
- **Weekly rhythm:** 1 product/new-stock post, 1 people or behind-the-scenes post, Stories when stock lands; GBP offer or event post when there is one; photos refreshed monthly.

### Sport business, low tolerance for headache
- **Networks:** Facebook (parents and adult members), Instagram, Google Business Profile. For under-16 members use WhatsApp, Messenger or email, per eSafety.
- **Weekly rhythm:** 1 results/community post, 1 people or session post (with consent, and mindful of child-safety policy); term dates and enrolments as GBP event or offer posts.
