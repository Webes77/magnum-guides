## 1. Australian Consumer Law and the ACCC

### 1.1 The basic rule
- Businesses must not make false or misleading claims on social media, and anything posted must be accurate and truthful. The ban on misleading or deceptive conduct applies to all advertising channels, social media included. **A** · https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/advertising-and-promotions/false-or-misleading-claims · https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/social-media-promotions
- The ACL's ban on misleading conduct applies whatever technology made the content, so AI-generated advertising is already covered. Treasury's review of AI and the ACL (final report, October 2025) is the government's own document on this. **A** for the report's existence and subject · https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-10/p2025-702329-fr.pdf · the plain-language "applies regardless of technology" line is from **B/C** summaries (Prosper Law, letspresent.com.au).

### 1.2 Penalties went up in 2026
- From 28 March 2026 the maximum penalty for a company breaching the ACL is the greatest of $100 million, three times the benefit gained, or 30% of adjusted turnover during the breach period. It was $50 million. **B** · K&L Gates (13 Apr 2026) https://www.klgates.com/Significant-Changes-for-Australian-Competition-and-Consumer-Laws-Doubling-of-Penalties-to-AU100-Million-Per-Offence-and-Unfair-Trading-Practices-to-Be-Prohibited-4-13-2026 · DW Fox Tucker (Apr 2026) https://www.dwfoxtucker.com.au/2026/04/penalties-increased-to-100million-for-breaches-australian-consumer-law · ACCC page https://www.accc.gov.au/business/compliance-and-enforcement/fines-and-penalties (not read).
- Course framing: the headline number is for big companies. For a small business the realistic consequence is an ACCC infringement notice (tens of thousands of dollars, see PhotobookShop below), a court order, or a platform removing the ad. Say "fines are real and recently went up", not "$100 million".

### 1.3 Comments on your own page are your responsibility
- A business is responsible for comments and posts other people make on its social media pages that are false or likely to mislead. A reply must be enough to override the false impression, so it is usually safer to remove the comment. **A** · https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/social-media-promotions
- Practical: check comments regularly, especially on a scheduled or automated post nobody is watching.

### 1.4 Reviews and testimonials
- Reviews must be independent and reflect the genuine opinion of someone who used the product or service. **A** · https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/online-reviews-for-product-and-services
- Writing fake reviews, or editing or removing genuine negative reviews to inflate your rating or hide criticism, breaches the ACL. **A** · same page.
- Businesses that do not remove reviews they know, or should know, are fake may be in breach. But you need an adequate basis before removing or editing a review as fake. **A** · same page.
- **PhotobookShop, March 2026.** Paid $39,600 after two ACCC infringement notices. It reposted an influencer's Instagram review with the critical parts ("a bit fiddly", "a bit confusing") edited out, and did not disclose the paid relationship. First ACCC financial penalty for undisclosed paid influencer reviews and for editing influencer content. **A** · https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/photobookshop-pays-penalties-for-influencer-reviews · date from **B** (Mallesons, HWL Ebsworth, SmartCompany snippets).
- Course lesson: trimming a real customer's words so they sound better is the same offence as inventing them.

### 1.5 AI-generated people and testimonials
- No ACCC page found that names "AI testimonial" as its own category. The rule follows from 1.4: a review must be the genuine opinion of a real person who used the service. An AI-written testimonial, or an AI-generated face presented as a customer, is not. **Inference from A** (online reviews page above). State it as a consequence of the review rule, not as a separate named law.
- The ACCC's 2025 to 2027 compliance priorities reportedly include influencer marketing, online reviews and AI-generated fake content. **C** (SafeAI-Aus / letspresent.com.au summary). Do not quote as ACCC wording.

### 1.6 Influencers, sponsored posts, gifted product
- The ACCC released guidance for influencers and businesses in early 2024 (after a December 2023 sweep finding 81% of 118 influencers reviewed raised concerns). **A** · https://www.accc.gov.au/about-us/publications/social-media-influencer-testimonials-and-endorsements · https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/scrutiny-of-influencers-and-businesses-for-misleading-advertising-and-online-reviews-continues
- Disclose a promotional post in a way that is immediately obvious, including when the influencer got free product, tickets or any gift or incentive. **A** (same) with **B** corroboration (Addisons, Bespoke Law).
- Clear labels: #ad, Advert, Advertising, Branded Content, Paid Partnership, Paid Promotion. Less clear and possibly not enough: #sp, Spon, gifted, Affiliate, Collab, "thanks to", or just naming the brand. **B** (law firm summaries of the ACCC guide).
- Businesses should never script an influencer to misrepresent their experience, and should require posts to be formatted so the disclosure is easy to notice. **B** (same).
- Relevance to the three businesses: the sports business giving a local athlete free gear, the retailer gifting product, the builder giving a discount for a post. All need the post labelled as an ad.

### 1.7 Before and after images
- No ACCC page specific to before and after photos was found for trades or retail. The general rule covers it: an image that misleads about what you did or what a customer will get is misleading conduct. The PhotobookShop case shows the ACCC treats editing to change the overall impression as the problem. **A by inference** (1.1, 1.4).
- Practical rule for the course: before and after photos must be the same job, taken by you, and edits must not change what the work looks like.

### 1.8 AI images of work not actually done
- No regulator page found on this exact scenario. It falls squarely under 1.1: a builder posting an AI render of a finished deck as if it were their job, or a retailer posting an AI image of a product that does not look like that, is a misleading representation. **Inference from A.** Safe pattern: label concept images as concepts ("AI concept render, not a completed job"), and use your own photos for claims about your work.

### 1.9 "Australian made" and country of origin
- A "Made in Australia" claim needs any imported components to have undergone a fundamental change in nature, identity or essential character here. Minor processing that only changes form or appearance is not enough. **A** · https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/advertising-and-promotions/country-of-origin-claims · https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-puts-businesses-on-notice-about-made-in-country-of-origin-claims
- Pictures count. A kangaroo, a map, a flag or an iconic national symbol can be a country of origin claim. **A** · same pages; ACCC PDF https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/Country%20of%20origin%20claims%20and%20the%20ACL_2022.pdf
- The green and gold kangaroo logo on non-food goods may only be used under licence from Australian Made Campaign Limited. **A** (ACCC snippet) / **C** (Wikipedia).
- AI trap: an image generator adding a flag or a kangaroo to a product shot can create a claim you did not intend.

## 2. Construction: licence numbers in advertising

### 2.1 Queensland (QBCC)
- If you hold a QBCC licence you must include your licence number in all your advertising, including on social media. **A** · https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/running-your-business/advertising
- The requirement is the name and QBCC licence number (for a company, the company's name and the company's licence number). **A** · same page.
- "Advertisements include social media pages (e.g. company Facebook pages)." **A** · same page (snippet). So the profile itself is an advertisement.
- Does every post need it? The QBCC page says "all your advertising, including on social media", and names the page as an advertisement. No official line was found saying a single organic post is or is not an advertisement on its own. **Safe course wording: put the licence number on every profile (bio, about section, cover) and in every post or ad that promotes building work.** Flag as the conservative reading, not a quoted rule.
- The rules apply to both licensees and unlicensed persons (an unlicensed person must not advertise building work they need a licence for). **A** (QBCC snippet) · https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/non-compliance/understand-when-you-are-breaking-law · **B** Jensen Lawyers https://www.jensenlawyers.com.au/post/advertising-requirements-when-you-don-t-hold-a-contractor-licence
- A business name or ABN is not a licence number. **A** · https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/news/australian-business-name-same-qbcc-licence-number
- Penalty: section 54 of the QBCC Act, maximum 35 penalty units, reported as about $6,044 for an individual and about $30,222 for a company. **B/C** (search summary, likely Jensen Lawyers). Queensland penalty unit values change each 1 July, so say "thousands of dollars per offence", not an exact figure.
- Electrical work is licensed separately under electrical safety law, with its own advertising requirements. **A** · https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/news-and-events/newsletters/esafe-newsletters/esafe-editions/esafe-electrical/2024-newsletters/april-2024/understanding-qbcc-and-electrical-workers-licences-and-advertising (not read beyond title and snippet).

### 2.2 New South Wales (if the construction client works across the border)
- The licensee's name and licence number must be shown in all advertising under the Home Building Act 1989. **A** · https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/running-a-business/advertising-laws-and-your-business/building-trade-advertisements
- Advertising residential building work over $5,000, or specialist work of any value, without a current contractor licence is prohibited. **A** (same, snippet) but the threshold wording could not be read in full. **Soften to "residential work above a small threshold".**
- Social media is not named explicitly in the snippet; "all advertising" is. **Inference.**

## 3. AI content: platform labels and Australian law

### 3.1 Meta (Facebook, Instagram, Threads)
- Meta labels AI content as "AI info". It applies the label when it detects industry-standard indicators (C2PA and IPTC metadata from tools like Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, Adobe, Midjourney, Shutterstock) or when the poster discloses. **A** · https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/ · https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/ · https://transparency.meta.com/governance/tracking-impact/labeling-ai-content/
- Meta requires people to use its AI disclosure tool when posting organic content with photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, and may apply penalties if they do not. Note: the organic requirement is for video and audio, not still images. **A** · same Meta pages.
- Ads: if Meta finds an advertiser has not disclosed as required, it rejects the ad; repeated failure may bring penalties. **A** (Meta snippet; the stated context is ads about social issues and politics) · https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/SIEP-advertising/SIEP/
- Claimed 2026 change: a global rule that all Facebook and Instagram advertisers must tick an "AI-generated" box in Ads Manager when AI creates or significantly alters images, video or audio, with Meta's own Advantage+ tools labelled automatically. **C only** (coinis.com, almcorp.com, cinerads.com). Not confirmed from a Meta page. Course wording: "if Ads Manager asks whether AI was used, answer honestly".

### 3.2 TikTok
- AI-generated content that shows realistic people or scenes must be labelled; TikTok uses C2PA Content Credentials to label automatically; ads containing AI visuals or audio use an AI disclosure setting in Ads Manager. Misleading AI content is prohibited. AI used only for scripts or hashtags does not need a label. **C** (storrito.com, cinerads.com, auditsocials.com). TikTok's own support page was blocked and not in results. Principle is consistent across sources; the exact mechanics are third-party.

### 3.3 YouTube
- Creators must disclose, via a Creator Studio setting, when realistic content (something a viewer could mistake for a real person, place, scene or event) is made with altered or synthetic media, including generative AI. Introduced March 2024. **A** · https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/
- Not required for clearly unrealistic or animated content, special effects, or AI used for scripts, ideas or captions. **A** · same.
- The label reads "Altered or synthetic content". **B/C** (third-party summaries).

### 3.4 LinkedIn
- LinkedIn shows a "CR" (Content Credentials) badge when an uploaded image or video carries C2PA metadata; clicking it shows the tool used and whether AI generated all or part of it. It does not detect AI itself, and no metadata means no badge. **B** · Fortune, 26 Nov 2024 https://fortune.com/2024/11/26/linkedin-content-credentials-labeling-challenges-c2pa · plus **C** blogs. No LinkedIn help page retrieved.

### 3.5 The common thread for the course
Every platform's rule turns on the same test: **could a viewer mistake it for a real photo, video or voice?** If yes, label it. AI help with the words of a caption does not need a label on any platform found.

### 3.6 Australian law on AI disclosure
- There is **no Australian law requiring a small business to label AI-generated social media content** as of 23 Sep 2026. Nothing found says otherwise.
- The National AI Centre published voluntary guidance, "Being clear about AI-generated content", in November 2025, recommending visible labels, watermarks and metadata scaled to risk. Voluntary, not enforceable. **B** · ABC News, 1 Dec 2025 https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-12-01/ai-guidance-label-watermark-ai-content/106083786 · Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer tracker https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/reports/ai-tracker/australia
- On 15 July 2026 the Prime Minister announced "AI in Australia's Interests": an Office of AI in PM&C and a plan to legislate mandatory Australian Standards for AI, with laws expected in Parliament in early 2027. Reported contents focus on data centres and copyright protection; no reporting found of a social media labelling duty for small business. **A** · https://www.pmc.gov.au/domestic-policy/office-ai · **B** · White & Case https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/australian-ai-update-australia-changes-course · Clayton Utz https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2026/july/from-principles-to-power-points-what-the-governments-ai-in-australias-interests-means-for-business
- Course wording: "No law makes you label AI posts yet. The platforms do, and misleading-conduct law already catches an AI image that fools a customer. Mandatory AI rules are coming, expected 2027."
- One narrow 2026 rule, not relevant to these clients: from Feb 2026 commercial radio must disclose synthetic AI hosts (ACMA code). **B/C** (SafeAI-Aus summary). Omit from the course.

## 4. Privacy and consent

### 4.1 Small business exemption: still there
- Businesses with annual turnover of $3 million or less are generally exempt from the Privacy Act's APPs. The exemption still stands. **B** (multiple law firm summaries, e.g. Stellar IP Law, Biztech Lawyers).
- On 31 August 2026 the Attorney-General released an exposure draft, the Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026 (submissions closed 18 Sep 2026). It **does not** remove the small business exemption. Legislation is expected in Parliament before the end of 2026. **A** · https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/privacy-reform/ · **B** · Clayton Utz Aug 2026 https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2026/august/the-next-wave-of-australian-privacy-reform-key-proposals-in-the-draft-personal-data-protection-bill-2026 · Johnson Winter Slattery https://jws.com.au/what-we-think/second-tranche-of-australian-privacy-act-reform-exposure-draft-legislation-key-proposals-and-practical-implications/
- Warning: several C-grade vendor blogs (epicit, weblegal, compliancekit) claim the exemption is "removed" or "now in force" in 2026. They are wrong on the evidence above. Do not use them.
- Exceptions exist (for example businesses that trade in personal information, or health service providers, can be covered regardless of turnover). **Not verified this session**; mention loosely: "some small businesses are covered anyway, check the OAIC's small business page".

### 4.2 The statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy
- In force since 10 June 2025 under the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024. Individuals can sue for a serious invasion of privacy, by intrusion into seclusion or by misuse of information. **A** · https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/more-privacy-rights/statutory-tort-for-serious-invasions-of-privacy · **B** · Bird & Bird https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/australia/4-things-to-know-about-australia's-new-statutory-tort-of-privacy
- **The small business exemption does not protect you from the tort.** It applies whatever your turnover. **B** (Lexology, Spruson & Ferguson, Cybernion).
- Threshold is high (serious, intentional or reckless, reasonable expectation of privacy, public interest balance). Course wording: "Posting an ordinary team photo will not get you sued. Posting a customer's home, a child, or someone's private moment without asking could."

### 4.3 Photos of customers, staff, children, homes and job sites
- Could not retrieve OAIC guidance on photos (search budget exhausted). What follows is practice, not quoted law:
  - Get written consent before posting an identifiable customer or staff member. A photo of an identifiable person is personal information.
  - For anyone under 18, consent from a parent or carer. For junior sport, follow the sport's own photo and digital media policy (see 7.2). eSafety says sport organisations should have a digital media policy covering what can be posted and how parent or carer consent is obtained. **A** · https://www.esafety.gov.au/communities/sport/what-do-social-media-age-restrictions-mean-for-sport-organisations
  - Client homes and job sites: remove house numbers, street signs, number plates, family photos, mail, and anything showing where someone lives or what they own. Ask the owner first. Security risk and tort risk, both. **Practice guidance, no source.**
  - Never feed a customer's photo into an AI tool to alter it without asking.

## 5. Copyright

### 5.1 AI-generated images
- Australian copyright needs a human author who contributed independent intellectual effort. An image made entirely by AI, even from your prompt, currently likely has no copyright. You can use it, but you may not be able to stop others copying it. **B/C** (Artificer Legal https://artificer.legal/copyright-ai-generated-content-australia, university library guides e.g. Newcastle, Notre Dame). No court has ruled squarely on AI images here; say "likely".
- The government has ruled out a text and data mining exception for AI training, and on 15 July 2026 said copyright holders' rights will be protected under the new AI framework. **B** · Hamilton Locke https://hamiltonlocke.com.au/no-free-pass-for-ai-australia-confirms-copyright-will-be-protected-under-new-mandatory-framework/
- Practical risk for a small business: an AI image that closely copies a real photo, artwork, logo or a famous character can still infringe someone else's copyright or trade mark. Don't prompt "in the style of [living artist]" or ask for brand logos.

### 5.2 Music in Reels and videos
- Business accounts on Instagram and Facebook are limited to the Meta Sound Collection (royalty-free, cleared for commercial use) or music they have licensed themselves; the main popular music library is licensed for personal, non-commercial use. **A** (Meta Business Help Centre, snippet) · https://www.facebook.com/business/help/402084904469945 · **C** detail (Foxi, Tripepi Smith).
- Sound Collection tracks are cleared for Facebook and Instagram only; reposting that Reel to TikTok, YouTube or LinkedIn does not carry the licence. **C.**
- TikTok's business equivalent (Commercial Music Library) was not researched this session.
- Course rule: business account, business music. Use the platform's commercial library or a paid licence. A trending song is not yours to use.

### 5.3 Other people's photos
- Someone else's photo is their copyright. Finding it on Google or Pinterest is not permission. Reposting a customer's photo needs their permission and credit. **No source retrieved this session**; this is basic Copyright Act 1968 practice. Low risk to state plainly.

## 6. Spam Act (DMs, texts, emails)

- A commercial electronic message needs the recipient's consent (express or inferred), must identify the sender with contact details, and must include a working unsubscribe that does not require logging in or giving more personal information. **A** · https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam
- Unsubscribes must be honoured within five business days. ACMA has fined businesses for missing this. **B** (DLA Piper, Norton Rose Fulbright).
- The Act covers email, SMS, MMS, push notifications, and instant and social media messages that promote goods or services. **B** · Ashurst https://www.ashurst.com/en/insights/acma-goes-ham-on-spam-is-your-business-in-breach-of-the-spam-act/ · Sparke Helmore https://www.sparke.com.au/insights/spam-in-the-spotlight-acma-raises-the-stakes-for-business-messaging/ · KWM https://www.kwm.com/global/en/insights/latest-thinking/spam-act-fine-for-social-media-links.html
- ACMA has fined large brands heavily (Kogan, Woolworths, Uber, Sportsbet, Ticketek and others). **B.**
- Course rule: replying to someone who messaged you first is fine. Cold-DMing strangers a sales pitch, or messaging a list of past customers without consent and an opt-out, is not. An automation that sends DMs must include a way to opt out.

## 7. Under-16 social media minimum age (in force 10 December 2025)

### 7.1 What the law does
- From 10 December 2025 age-restricted platforms must take reasonable steps to stop Australians under 16 from having accounts. **A** · https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions · https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/media-communications/internet/online-safety/social-media-minimum-age
- eSafety's view is that Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, Threads, TikTok, Twitch, X, YouTube, Kick and Reddit are age-restricted, among others. **A** · https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions/which-platforms-are-age-restricted
- Messaging apps, online gaming, professional networking, and education and health services are not covered. **A** (same hub, snippet).
- The obligation sits with the platforms. Ordinary businesses do not have to verify the age of people who view or follow their page. **B** · Sprintlaw https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/under-16-social-media-ban/

### 7.2 What it means for a sports business with junior players
- Sport organisations can still post photos and videos of under-16 participants on sport-run accounts, as long as those accounts are run by staff, volunteers or members aged 16 or over. **A** · https://www.esafety.gov.au/communities/sport/what-do-social-media-age-restrictions-mean-for-sport-organisations
- If content shows anyone under 16, follow the sport's own policies on photos, video and livestreaming of children, as for anyone under 18; the organisation's digital media policy should say how parent or carer consent is obtained. **A** · same page.
- Under-16 athletes cannot legally hold accounts on restricted platforms, and there is no exemption for commercial or sponsored use. **B** · AusTriathlon https://www.triathlon.org.au/featured/what-the-new-under-16-social-media-laws-mean-for-junior-athletes/ · AFL South East club guidance (Dec 2025).
- Practical consequences for the course:
  1. Kids are no longer your audience on Instagram or TikTok. Their parents are. Write for parents.
  2. Don't ask junior players to follow, tag, share or enter a competition on a restricted platform. They are not supposed to be there.
  3. You can't tag an under-16 because they should not have an account. Don't name them in captions without parent consent either.
  4. For reaching families directly, use channels outside the ban (email with consent, a club app, a messaging group run by parents), and the Spam Act rules in section 6 apply.
  5. Nobody under 16 on staff runs the business account.

## 8. One-page checklist: before you post

Plain rules for a beginner. Twelve, in the order a post is made.

1. **Is it true?** Every claim, price, "best", "cheapest", "Australian made" and every picture must match what the customer actually gets.
2. **Is that our work?** Only show jobs, products and results that are really yours. If an AI image is a concept, say "concept" on it.
3. **Could someone mistake it for a real photo or video?** If AI made or changed it, switch on the platform's AI label. When Ads Manager asks, answer honestly.
4. **No made-up people.** No AI customers, no AI testimonials, no reviews written by you or your staff, no trimming a real review to make it nicer.
5. **Paid, gifted or discounted?** If anyone got something for the post, it says #ad or Paid Partnership, where people see it first.
6. **Builders: licence number on.** QBCC licence number (and the licensed name) on every profile, and on every post or ad selling building work. NSW work: NSW licence number too.
7. **Did they say yes?** Written OK from every identifiable customer or staff member in the photo. Parent or carer consent for anyone under 18.
8. **Hide the address.** Crop out house numbers, street signs, number plates, mail and personal items at client homes and job sites. Ask the owner.
9. **Is the music ours to use?** Business account means the platform's commercial library or music you paid for. Not the trending song.
10. **Is the photo ours?** Your own photos, stock you licensed, or permission and credit. Not Google Images. Don't ask AI for other brands' logos or a living artist's style.
11. **Juniors: parents are the audience.** Never ask under-16s to follow, tag or enter anything on a social platform, and follow the sport's photo policy.
12. **Messages: ask first.** Only DM or text a sales message to people who agreed to hear from you, and always give a way to opt out.

And after posting: **check the comments.** A false claim in a comment on your page is treated as yours.
