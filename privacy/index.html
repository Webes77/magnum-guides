What is kept, and who can see it

Three different questions get treated as one. Whether what you type is stored, whether it is used to train the model, and whether a person can read it. They have different answers and different switches. This page gives you all three, then the settings to change today.

01 · What happens to what you type

Three questions, not one

Your message leaves your device, travels to the company's servers, gets processed there, and the answer comes back. That is true of every one of these tools, on every plan, including the ones that call themselves private. Nothing is processed on your laptop.

From there, three things happen independently, and almost everyone collapses them into a single worry.

Is it stored

Almost always yes, for a period. The period changes with your plan and with one toggle. Deleting a chat removes it from your screen straight away, and from the company's systems within thirty days, not instantly.

Is it used to train

On a personal Claude or ChatGPT account, yes by default. On a business or enterprise account, no by default. This is the one you can turn off in about fifteen seconds, and it is the one that changes how long things are kept.

Can a person read it

The one people never ask about, and the one that actually bites. On a work account the answer is usually yes, an administrator can retrieve it, and no setting you control changes that.

The catch most people missDeleting a chat does not delete what the tool remembered from it. Memory is a separate store with its own list and its own delete button. Section 04 covers both.

03 · Who can see it

Private from your sidebar is not private from your employer

This is the part worth reading twice, because the wording of the feature actively encourages the wrong conclusion.

An incognito chat in Claude is not saved to your history and is not used for training. Both true. What it is not is invisible. It is still kept for thirty days, or longer if your organisation's retention policy says so. And on a Team or Enterprise plan, incognito chats are included in the organisation data export that account Owners can run, and in the Compliance API on Enterprise.

So if you are on a work account and you open an incognito chat to ask something you would rather your employer did not see, you have hidden it from yourself and from nobody else.

On a personal account

  • Nobody at your workplace can pull your chats.
  • Staff access at the company is limited and audited, but not zero.
  • A legal request or a breach is still a real path, whatever your settings say.

On a work account

  • Assume an administrator can retrieve anything you type.
  • Incognito and temporary chats are included in that.
  • On ChatGPT Enterprise and Edu the compliance tools cover conversations, files and memories.

ChatGPT Business is slightly different in the detail: an administrator does not automatically get a window into your ordinary chats, but Enterprise and Edu workspaces have a compliance platform that records conversations, uploaded files, memories and users. The practical advice does not change. On an account your employer pays for, write as though it can be read back to you.

04 · What to switch off

Ten minutes, once

Do these in order. The first one in each list is the one that matters most, and none of them costs you anything you will notice in daily use.

In Claude

  1. Turn off model training

    Settings › Privacy › Help improve Claude

    Switch it off. This stops new chats being used for training, and it drops retention from up to five years back to thirty days. It is the single highest-value switch on this page.

  2. Turn off or pause memory

    Settings › Memory › Generate memory from chats

    Switch it off if you do not want it building a profile of you across chats. Pausing keeps what is already stored but stops it being used or added to.

  3. Read what it already remembers, and delete

    Settings › Memory › Topics

    Turning memory off does not delete what is already there. Open the list, read it properly, and delete anything naming a client, a price, a contract or a person who is not you. Reset clears the lot.

    The prompt at the foot of this page gets Claude to summarise what it is holding, so you have something to check that list against.

  4. Use an incognito chat for the genuinely sensitive one-off

    Useful on a personal account. On a work account, read section 03 first, because it does not do what the name suggests.

Say where this came fromThe two memory paths above are third-party reporting, not something confirmed against Anthropic's own help pages from here. The menus move. If what you see does not match, the setting still exists under Settings, so go looking rather than assuming it is gone.

In ChatGPT

  1. Turn off model training

    Settings › Data Controls › Improve the model for everyone

    Switch it off. New conversations still appear in your history, they are just no longer used to train the model.

  2. Turn off memory

    Settings › Personalization › Memory

    Switch it off and it stops creating and using memories.

  3. Clear what it has already saved

    Settings › Personalization › Manage memory

    Same trap as Claude. Turning memory off leaves the existing memories in place, and deleting a chat does not delete the memories that chat created. Open the list and delete them yourself.

  4. Use a Temporary Chat for the one-offs

    The Temporary button sits at the top right of a new chat. It stays out of your history, creates no memories and is not used for training. It is still kept thirty days.

05 · What never to put in

The settings do not cover everything

Every switch in section 04 controls training and retention. None of them controls what happens if the company receives a legal request, if there is a breach, or if somebody gets into your own account. Deletion is not instant either. So some things are better not typed at all.

Never paste

  • Passwords, API keys, bank details, anything that is a credential.
  • Tax file numbers, Medicare numbers, passport or licence numbers.
  • Anything under an NDA you have not read recently.
  • A customer database, an export, or a whole list of people's details.
  • Health information or employee records about a named person.

Fine to paste

  • Your own drafts, emails and notes.
  • A quote or a proposal with the client's name taken out.
  • Numbers with the identifying detail stripped, which is usually one find and replace.
  • Anything already published.

The test that settles most of itIf this exact message were read out in a dispute, or landed in a journalist's inbox, would it cost you a client, a contract or a relationship? If yes, take the names out first. That takes about ten seconds and removes most of the risk on this page.

Third parties are the part people forget. Your own risk is yours to take. Pasting a client's contract, a supplier's pricing or an employee's medical certificate is a decision you are making on behalf of somebody who never agreed to it.

06 · If you only do three things

Three things

  1. Turn the training toggle off in both tools. Under a minute each, and on Claude it cuts retention from five years to thirty days.
  2. Open the memory list and read it. Not the toggle, the list. Delete anything naming a client, a price or a person who is not you.
  3. On a work account, assume an administrator can read it, incognito and temporary chats included. Write accordingly.

The prompt · Audit what it remembers

See what it is holding about you

Run this in Claude or ChatGPT. It does not replace the memory list in your settings, it gives you something to check that list against, in plain sentences rather than a wall of fragments. The settings list is the authoritative one and the only place you can actually delete.

You are auditing what you have stored about me, so I can decide
what should not be there.

I run a small business. Anything you remember about me can turn up
in future answers, and some of it may be commercially sensitive or
about other people who never agreed to it.

Work only from what you actually have stored. Do not guess, do not
infer from this conversation, and do not fill in gaps. If you cannot
see your own stored memory, say so plainly and stop.

Answer in this exact format:
WHAT YOU REMEMBER: every item, grouped by topic, in plain sentences.
SHOULD NOT BE THERE: any item naming a client, a person who is not
me, a price, a contract, a login, or a health or financial detail.
One line each, with why it is a problem.
SAFE TO KEEP: the rest, in one line.

Stop after that list.

Then open the memory list in your settings and delete from there. The prompt tells you what to look for. It cannot delete anything itself.

This prompt is on the Prompt Shelf under Setting up, so it is one copy away next time.
