Set Claude up to handle your business admin

Anthropic has a free add-on for Claude built for small businesses. Install it once, tell it about your business, and it writes your Monday numbers, chases the people who owe you money and turns a call into a quote. Claude does the work. You approve anything that touches money or customers. About 20 minutes, start to finish.

The short version

Six stages, about 20 minutes, top to bottom

The rules that never bend

  1. Nothing is sent, posted or paid until you say yes.
  2. Claude drafts. You read it, and you send it.
  3. Check every figure against your own records before it goes to a customer.
  4. No tool has to be connected to start. Connected tools make it deeper.

Before you start

A paid plan, and know which tools you use

  1. Any paid Claude plan. The plugin itself costs nothing extra.
  2. Claude on the web or the desktop app. Set it up on a computer. You can read the results on your phone afterwards.
  3. The names of your tools. What you use for accounting, email and calendar. Claude asks for them by name.

Stage 1 · Install it

Two minutes, one time

  1. Open Claude. Use claude.ai in your browser, or the Claude desktop app.
  2. Click Customize in the left sidebar.
  3. Click Plugins.
  4. Search for small-business.
  5. Click Small Business. Make sure it says it is by Anthropic. Other plugins have similar names.
  6. Click Install. The button can also say Add.
  7. Skip the connections for now if Claude asks. Stage 3 does them.

If your screen looks different. Claude's menus have changed more than once this year. If you cannot see Customize, look for Plugins in Settings or in the menu beside your name. The name to find is always Small Business, by Anthropic.

Stage 2 · Make it yours

Tell it about your business once

Out of the box it is written for an average small business. This step rewrites it for yours, so every job after this sounds like you and knows how you work.

  1. Start a new chat.
  2. Copy the prompt below and paste it in.
  3. Answer each question Claude asks. It asks one at a time.
  4. Read the file card it shows you at the end. Check it describes your business correctly.
  5. Click Save plugin. If you do not save it, the changes are lost.

Prompt 2.1 · customise it

Make the plugin yours

Customize the "small-business" plugin for me based on my company.

What it asks, and how to answer well

It asks what you do, who works with you, and what is hardest right now. The answers decide how good every job after this is, so give it more than you think it needs.

What you do

Thin“Plumbing.”

Useful“Residential plumbing on the Gold Coast. Mostly hot water and bathroom renos. Jobs run $400 to $25,000. Customers find us on Google.”

Who works with you

Thin“Four of us.”

Useful“Me, two plumbers and my partner, who does the books two days a week. I do all the quoting.”

What is hardest

Thin“Admin.”

Useful“Quotes take me two nights a week. About $15,000 is always overdue. I never know my cash position until the bookkeeper tells me.”

  • Name your tools. “Xero and Outlook”, not “accounting software”.
  • Give real numbers where you know them: job size, how many quotes a week, what is overdue.
  • Say what never goes out without you. For example: “Nothing goes to a customer until I have read it.”
  • Talk instead of type if it is quicker. Press the microphone and answer as you would to a new office manager.

Stage 3 · Connect two tools

Accounting and email first

This is the step that changes it from “paste it in” to “Claude goes and gets it”. Two tools cover all three jobs on this page.

  1. Open the Small Business plugin in your list of plugins.
  2. Open its Connectors tab.
  3. Click Connect beside your accounting software.
  4. Sign in with your usual login for that software.
  5. Click Allow.
  6. Do steps 3 to 5 again for your email. Gmail is on the plugin's list. If you use Outlook, connect Microsoft 365 in your own Claude connectors instead. The plugin uses it from there.

Already connected? If you connected Xero or Gmail to Claude before, you may see it twice: once in your own connectors and once on the plugin's tab. It is the same tool. Either one works. You do not need to connect it again.

Your accounting software is not on the list? In a chat, type “Connect to” and the name of your software. Claude checks for a connector and, if there is none, offers to connect it through Zapier.

Your week on one page

This is the one to run first. It uses everything you just connected, and it shows you in two minutes whether the setup works.

  1. Start a new chat.
  2. Copy the command below and paste it in. Then send it.
  3. Read the page it makes.
  4. Check the cash figure against your bank or your accounting software.

Stage 5 · Chase your overdue invoices

It writes the reminders. You send them.

The ruleClaude drafts every reminder. You read every one. You send it. Nothing goes to a customer until you have read it.
  1. Start a new chat.
  2. Copy the prompt below and paste it in.
  3. Answer the one setup question if it asks which email you use.
  4. Read the summary table. It lists each customer, the amount, the days late and the tone of the reminder.
  5. Check each amount against your accounting software.
  6. Read each draft in full. Change any line that does not sound like you.
  7. Tell Claude which drafts to put in your email. They go to your drafts folder, not to the customer.
  8. Open your drafts folder and send each one yourself.

Prompt 5.1 · the chase

Chase what you are owed

Chase my overdue invoices. Draft the reminders, don't send them.

Why the second sentence. The plugin already waits for your yes. Saying it in the prompt makes it the first thing Claude reads, and it covers the one exception: invoices sent through PayPal, where the reminder can go out from PayPal itself once you approve.

What it does before it writes a word

  • Checks for payments already made. A customer who paid in the last two weeks is marked “possibly paid, verify” and gets no reminder. Check those before you chase.
  • Matches the tone to the customer. Gentle for good payers, firmer for repeat late payers.
  • Puts all of a customer's overdue invoices in one email, so nobody gets three reminders in a morning.

Stage 6 · Turn a call into a proposal

From the conversation to a quote they can sign

The two nights a week spent writing quotes is the job this saves most often. It works from whatever you have after the call.

  1. Get what came out of the call. A transcript, the text of a voice note, photos of the job, or a few lines of notes. Most phones turn a voice memo into text for you.
  2. Start a new chat and attach it.
  3. Copy the prompt below and paste it in the same message. If you attached a voice note or photos, change “call transcript” to say so.
  4. Answer its questions. It lists what is missing, such as a measurement or a start date, and asks rather than guesses.
  5. Check the price, the scope and the dates. It shows which past jobs it priced from, so you can check the number quickly.
  6. Send the proposal yourself. It comes back as a Word document and a PDF.

Prompt 6.1 · the proposal

Build the proposal

Build a proposal from this call transcript.

No past invoices connected? Attach your price list or rate card as well. It prices from what you have actually charged, not from a general guess, so it needs one or the other.

What else it can do

The jobs worth trying next

There are over forty jobs in the plugin. These are the ones that suit a business of one to ten people. You do not need the names: say what you need in plain words and Claude picks the job.

Money

  • “Cash forecast” Your cash for the next 30, 60 and 90 days, with the risks named.
  • “Pay the bills” Reads and codes your bills, checks your cash, and lines up a payment run for you to approve.
  • “Close the books” Checks the month against your payments and writes what the month means.

Customers

  • “Respond to this lead” Drafts a reply to each new enquiry, with real times you are free.
  • “Who should I call today?” The five people most worth a call, with what to say.
  • “Win back quiet customers” Finds customers who have stopped buying and drafts a note to each.
  • “Update my CRM” Keeps your customer list current from your email and calendar.
  • “Review this contract” Reads a contract and flags the terms to question, in plain English.

Marketing

  • “What should I promote?” A 30-day plan from what is actually selling.
  • “Give me a month of posts” Posts in your voice, held for you to approve before anything goes live.
  • “What are people saying about us?” Pulls your reviews together and drafts a reply to each one.
  • “How do I show up in ChatGPT?” Checks whether Google and AI assistants can find your website.

Team and stock

  • “Write a job post” The ad, the interview questions and the offer letter.
  • “Screen these applications” A shortlist scored against what the job needs.
  • “What do I need to reorder?” What to order and when, from what is selling.
  • “Go through my email” Sorts your inbox into needs you, drafted, and done.

Leave two alone for now. The payroll and tax jobs are written for the United States. For Australian payroll, BAS and tax, keep using your bookkeeper or accountant.

When the standard version isn't enough

Two things to try, then a custom build

The plugin is built for the average small business. Before you decide it does not fit, try two things it does itself.

  1. Say “Connect to” and the name of your tool. It connects software it does not know, through Zapier.
  2. Say “Automate this” after a job you repeat. It turns that job into one of its own.

If it still does not fit how your business works, that is where a custom build comes in. Your own quoting rules, a job system nothing connects to, or three tools that need to talk to each other. I scope and quote those separately. Ask me when you are ready.
