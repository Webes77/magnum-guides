01 · The business

Who Magnum AI serves

Magnum AI is a one-person consultancy on the Gold Coast, Queensland.

I help small businesses perform better by building the AI systems, skills, and habits that make their people stronger operators.

Magnum AI works with owner-run businesses with roughly one to ten staff. Clients include businesses in joinery, commercial furniture, civil contracting, hospitality, registered training organisations, NDIS providers, health and wellness.

They are practical people who are already using AI without getting results from it.

The 6 Lever Framework

The Magnum AI 6 Lever Framework is the proprietary method used across the business.

Role · Context · Constraints · Tone · Format · Output

The six levers are always used in this order. They are never renamed, reordered, added to or reduced.

02 · Brand character

What it is, and what it is not

What it is

  • Industrial. Engineered. Hard edged.
  • Closer to a tool stamp or a workshop plate than to a software company.
  • Confident without shouting.
  • Plain rather than decorated.

What it is not

  • Corporate.
  • Soft.
  • Decorative.
  • Styled like a software company.

The gate

Would it survive being printed in black and white on a bad photocopier? If it only works in colour, or only works large, it is not strong enough. Every element in this system has to pass that, and the mark most of all.

03 · The mark

The mark

A heavy condensed letter M with the letters AI nested in its central valley, so the mark reads as Magnum containing AI.

The AI sits on the same baseline as the feet of the M and is set in coral. The badge lockup places that mark inside a circle: a heavy ring, MAGNUM arced across the top, AI straight across the bottom, and a short coral bar at three o'clock and nine o'clock separating the two arcs. No other text appears in the badge.

The artwork was designed and delivered in September 2026 and the copyright is transferred. The files are the only artwork. Never redraw the mark, never trace it, never rebuild it from a screenshot.

The three lockups

Approved colourways

The navy and coral reverses are one colour: the AI reverses with everything else rather than staying coral. That is what shipped and it is correct for one colour application. The two colour reverse, where the AI stays bright coral, exists for the mark alone.

The name in text

Magnum AI. No full stops, no punctuation between the letters, AI always capitalised. Never "Magnum A.I." and never "MagnumAI".

04 · Clear space and size

Clear space

Clear space on every side equals the stroke width of the M. Nothing sits inside it. No text, no rule, no image edge, no other logo.

Dashed line = the clear space boundary · X = the stroke width of the M, on all four sides

05 · Misuse

Logo misuse

Use only the approved lockups and colourways. Every treatment below is wrong.

  • Do not separate the AI from the M.
  • Do not redraw the mark in a different typeface.
  • Do not present the mark on an effect mockup: minted coin, branded leather, embossed panel, foil stamp.
06 · Colour

One accent, two values

White paper, one navy block per screen, coral as the only accent.

Coral carries two values and the difference is load-bearing. Display coral is 3.73:1 on paper, so it carries type only at 18px and up, where size does the contrast work. Anything smaller a person reads takes text coral at 5.02:1.

07 · Typography

Three faces, one job each

Never a fourth. Never Playfair Display, Inter, Bebas Neue, Archivo Narrow or Roboto.

Display · Oswald

Build the system, not the prompt

600 or 700 · uppercase · line height .90 to 1.12 · headings, card titles, big numbers · fallback Arial Narrow

Text · IBM Plex Sans

Running text sits at 17px on a page and 15.5px inside a card. Emphasis is weight 500 or 600, never italic, because Oswald has no italic in this system and a slant would not match it.

400 running · 500 and 600 emphasis · fallback Arial

Utility · IBM Plex Mono

What we covered · 9 September 2026

400 or 500 · 11 to 13px · uppercase · letter spaced .14em to .28em · labels, dates, metadata, footers · fallback Courier New

The fallbacks matter. A deck opened on forty machines cannot depend on a web font being installed, so anything rendered in PowerPoint, Word or a PDF without embedded fonts uses Arial Narrow, Arial and Courier New.

08 · Layout and components

Built from four parts

A page is a navy block, a label, a grid of cards and a figure plate. Nothing else is needed and nothing else is invented.

Tag and badge

FoundationsSystems buildRetainerIncluded

A tag classifies and may repeat. A badge flags one thing and may not. Two badges on a surface and neither means anything.

  • A card that links somewhere turns its border coral on hover and focus. A static card does not change, because movement on something unclickable is a lie.
  • Cards within a section are all of one kind: all with images, or all without.
  • Never ship a placeholder card. An empty section beats advertising an empty room.
  • If a layout feels full, cut content rather than shrink the type.

Hard edges

No rounded corners. No drop shadows. No gradients. No textures, dot grids or pattern backgrounds. Structure comes from the 2px ink border and the navy block, never from softness or depth.

09 · Icons

One weight, one colour

Icons carry a number, a label or a step. They are never decoration and they never appear alone.

Every icon is drawn by hand as inline SVG on a 48 by 48 grid, stroke only at 2.4, round caps and joins, five paths or fewer. Never imported from a library.

10 · Imagery

Image light, not image free

Every layout needs one thing to look at, in this order of preference.

Use, in order

  • A real screenshot, cropped tight, no browser chrome or device mockup.
  • A single weight line icon or diagram, one per component.
  • Photography of real work, real sites, real hands on real tools.
  • A line figure on a navy plate.

Never use

  • Stock photography.
  • Circuit boards and neural networks.
  • Glowing blue anything and abstract AI gradients.
  • 3D renders and isometric business people.
  • An image chosen because a layout looked empty.

If nothing real exists, the answer is white space.

11 · Voice and hard rules

How it reads

Direct. Confident. Plain, present tense, matter of fact. A smart peer, not a service provider.

First person throughout, never third person. Lead with the answer and put the reasoning after it. An agenda states what will be covered, not what someone walks out with.

  • No slogans, taglines, straplines or descriptor lines. There is not one and there is not going to be one.
  • No em dashes, anywhere, including alt text, speaker notes, prompts and file names. Use a comma, a full stop, a colon or a middot.
  • Never the word "solid" in prose.
  • No future scenario framing. No "imagine if", no "picture this", no "three months from now".
  • Prices are quoted plus GST, never inclusive.
  • No client names on anything that ships beyond that client.
  • Never change a URL that has already been sent to a client, including in a redesign.
  • Never restyle a document a client has already received.

Two live things sit deliberately outside this system: the Fine-Tune guide in the members area, and This Week in AI, the newsletter, which is a separate product with its own look.
