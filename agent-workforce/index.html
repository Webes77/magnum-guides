Build your own agent workforce

I run my business with a small team of AI agents. Each one has one job, turns up on its shift, and leaves its work as a draft for me to approve. This page is how to hire your own, in Claude Code, one agent at a time, starting with the one everybody should hire first.

The short version

Six stages, top to bottom, one prompt at a time

The hiring plan

  1. Set up the office
  2. Hire your chief of staff
  3. Hire your two checkers
  4. Move your old schedules onto the team
  5. First day for your first specialist
  6. Roll call and performance review

The rules that never bend

  1. Your chief of staff is always hire number one. It reads, it never touches.
  2. Every agent reads Team HQ, the shared memory, before it starts.
  3. Drafts only. Nothing is posted, sent or published until you say yes.
  4. Nothing old is switched off until your auditor says PASS.
  5. When it gets something wrong, fix one thing. Never restart the recipe.

What an agent is

A job, a desk and a shift. That is the whole thing.

A chatbot waits for you to ask. An agent has a job, a place to work and a start time, and does the job without being asked.

Every agent anyone sells you is the same three parts with a different label on the front. Once you can see them, you can build one.

The skill

The recipe

The written steps for one job. Saved once, run the same way every time.

The folder

The desk

One place it reads from and one place it leaves its work. Usually your inbox, your files and a Drafts folder.

The schedule

The shift

The day and time it clocks on. Friday 7am. Monday 8am. It turns up whether you remember or not.

How one shift runs

  1. On shiftIt clocks on
  2. ReadsIts desk and Team HQ
  3. WorksFollows the recipe
  4. CheckedVoice and facts
  5. DraftsWaits for you
  6. YouApprove

Everything up to the draft happens without you. Nothing leaves the building until you have read it and said yes.

Meet my team

Seven agents, one of me

This is the team I run Magnum AI with. My first hire was a chief of staff. Every morning it reads my inbox, calendar and meeting notes and tells me the three things that matter. Everything else came later, one job at a time.

  • PercyChief of staff

    A 6am brief and a 4pm sweep of my email, calendar and meetings, plus a Sunday brief on the week in AI. Where a job can be done safely as a draft, it does it and tells me, so the brief is not a to-do list. Never sends anything.

  • PennyOperations

    Invoices, reminders and holiday checks.

  • RhettClients

    Client health and the weekly numbers.

  • RemyMarketing

    The weekly newsletter.

  • MaxGuides

    The guides and training material.

  • QuinnQuality

    Checks everything before I see it, against twelve of my real emails and a list of lines that sound like AI to me. Its only job is to find the mistake first.

  • ArchieFiling

    Tidies my desktop every Friday at 4pm. The one agent that runs on my computer rather than in the cloud, because that is where the files are.

Giving them names is not a gimmick. “Ask Penny” is quicker to say than “run the invoice reminder skill”, and a name makes it obvious when two agents are doing the same job.

Your org chart

Fill in the blanks as you hire

This is where you are heading. You sit at the top. One chief of staff reports to you. Two checkers keep everybody honest. Specialists get added one at a time, each owning one job you repeat every week. Underneath all of them is Team HQ, the one file every agent reads before it starts.

House rules

Five rules every agent follows, and two checkers who enforce them

Drafts, never posts

Nothing is posted, sent or published without you. An agent that can send on its own is an agent that can embarrass you on its own.

Read HQ first

Every agent opens Team HQ before it starts, so nobody repeats last week or raises something you already dealt with.

Checked before seen

The quality checker reads every draft before it lands in front of you. You should be the second pair of eyes, not the first.

One job each

New repeat job, new agent. Same recipe, desk and shift pattern. An agent with three jobs does all three badly.

Done, not to-do

A report opens with what the agent already did, then the few things only you can do. If it can do a job safely as a draft, it does it rather than adding it to your list. A brief that is all homework just leaves you feeling behind.

Why two checkers, not one

The checkerWhat it checksWhen
Quality checkerThe work. Does it sound like you, has it been done before, are the dates and names and prices right, and has nothing been sent.Every piece of work, every shift
AuditorThe team. Did a change miss anything, lose anything, or get the schedule wrong, and is every agent still drafts only.Every time you add, rebuild, move or retire an agent

The quality checker stops a bad draft. The auditor stops a bad change, which is the more expensive mistake, because a broken agent keeps being broken every week until somebody notices. So one rule sits above the rest: nothing old is switched off until the auditor says PASS.

Stage 1 · Set up the office

Why this happens in Code, and what to have ready

Claude Code is the Code tab in the same Claude app you already use. Same Claude, but it builds things that keep working after you close the chat. No coding skills needed: you talk to it in plain English and it writes whatever is needed underneath.

Chat

You ask, it answers

Great for thinking and drafting. Nothing happens once you stop typing.

Task work

It does a job

Reads and changes the files you give it, one job at a time, while you are there. The Task Work guide covers it.

Code

It builds the staff

Writes the recipes, sets up the desks and puts them on shifts. This is where agents live.

Code in sixty seconds

  1. Open it. The Claude desktop app, the Code tab, then choose the folder you made for your team.
  2. Ask in plain English. Paste the prompt. It writes any code it needs. You never have to.
  3. Approve the big steps. It asks before it connects, saves or schedules anything. Read what it is asking, then allow it.
  4. Ask what it did. “Explain what you just built in plain English.” You read the result, not the code.

What to have ready

  1. A paid Claude plan and the desktop app, open on the Code tab.
  2. Your email, calendar and files connected on your Claude account at claude.ai, under connectors. A shift that runs in the cloud can only reach what is connected there.
  3. One folder for your team on your computer, with a Drafts folder inside it. Stage 2 puts Team HQ in it.
  4. One job you repeat every week. Write it down now. It becomes your first specialist.

Where a shift runs: your computer or the cloud

This is the one technical choice in the whole guide, and it decides whether an agent turns up when you are on holiday.

Local scheduled taskCloud routine
Runs whenYour computer is on and awake, and the Claude app is openAny time, with your computer off
Can readFiles on your computer, plus your connectorsYour connectors only. It cannot see files on your computer
Good forJobs that need your local files, while you are at your deskAnything that reads email, calendar or Google Drive

Ask for a cloud routine by default. In Code, type /schedule, or just say “set this up as a cloud routine” in the prompt, which every scheduling prompt below already does. If a job needs something the cloud cannot reach, Claude will tell you, and you choose between moving the files to Google Drive or keeping it as a local task.

The catch nobody mentionsTeam HQ is a file. If your shifts run in the cloud and Team HQ sits on your computer, the cloud cannot read it. Keep Team HQ in Google Drive, connected on your Claude account, and every agent can reach it wherever it runs.

Stage 2 · Hire number one

Your chief of staff reads everything and touches nothing

Always the first hire, for two reasons. It is read only, so it cannot break anything while you learn. And the brief it writes is the fastest way to find out which jobs you should hand over next. Three prompts: a test run, the shared memory, then its first shift.

Prompt 2.1 · the interview

Ten minutes as your chief of staff

The moment it clicks. Read what it finds before you move on.

Act as my chief of staff for ten minutes. My business is [BUSINESS NAME]. Read only: don't send, change or delete anything, and leave anything private or personal out of the brief.
Look at my email from the last 7 days (skip newsletters and promotions), my calendar for the next 7 days, and my files changed in the last 7 days.
Then give me one short brief:
1. Top 3 things that need me this week, and why now
2. Who's waiting on a reply from me, and for how long
3. Money owed to me or due from me
4. Anything slipping or forgotten
5. One job I could hand to an agent every week
If I sent the last email in a thread, it's waiting on them, not me. Keep it under 250 words, in plain English.

Why the last line matters. Without it, every thread where you are waiting on somebody else turns up as something you owe. That one sentence is the difference between a useful brief and a guilt list.

Prompt 2.2 · the shared memory

Name it, and build Team HQ

You pick the name. Team HQ is what stops agents repeating themselves or forgetting what you decided.

I'm naming my chief of staff [CHIEF OF STAFF NAME]. Now give my team a shared memory.
Create a file called "Team HQ" in [this folder, or my Google Drive if my shifts will run in the cloud], with these sections:
1. Who we are: one paragraph from my files
2. Voice: how I write, with a few real examples
3. The team: each agent's name and job
4. Hard rules: nothing is posted, sent or published without me approving it
5. Work done: a dated list of what each agent produced, newest first
6. Handled: things I've dealt with, so no agent raises them again
From now on every agent on my team reads Team HQ before it starts, and adds to "Work done" and "Handled" when it finishes.

Prompt 2.3 · the first shift

Put it on a Monday shift

Your first scheduled agent.

Turn the brief you just gave me into a skill called monday-brief, run by [CHIEF OF STAFF NAME]. Every run it must:
1. Read Team HQ first and skip anything under "Handled"
2. Check my last 7 days of email, the next 7 days of my calendar, and anything new in my Drafts folder
3. Tell me my top 3 for the week, who's waiting on me, money owed or due, anything my agents left in Drafts, and anything slipping
4. Treat a thread as waiting on them, not me, if I sent the last email
5. Before telling me something hasn't been done, check my Sent Items and my replies, not just my calendar
6. Never send, reply, delete or change anything
Then schedule it every Monday at 8am [your time zone] as a cloud routine, so it runs with my computer off. If a cloud routine can't reach something it needs, tell me what, and we'll decide whether to set it up as a local scheduled task instead. Let me know when it's ready.

If it has to run locally. Leave your computer on and the Claude app open at 8am on Monday, or it will skip the shift.

Stage 3 · Hire your checkers

Two hires whose only job is to find the mistake first

Do these before anything else gets built, so every piece of work and every change from here on gets checked. Quinn does the first job on my team. Choose names that are easy to say, because you will say them a lot.

Prompt 3.1 · reads every piece of work

The quality checker

Build a skill called team-quality-check and name the checker [CHECKER NAME]. Every agent on my team runs it on its work before saving anything. Assume there is at least one mistake and find it:
1. Voice: does it sound like me, or like a generic AI?
2. Repeats: has any of this been done before? Check "Work done" in Team HQ
3. Facts: dates, names, places and prices match my files and calendar
4. Safety: nothing has been posted, sent or published
Return PASS or a numbered list of fixes, apply them, then check once more.
Add this check as the last step of monday-brief, and add [CHECKER NAME] to "The team" in Team HQ.

Prompt 3.2 · checks every change to the team

The auditor

It never does the work itself. It checks that the team is built the way you asked.

Build a second checker called [AUDITOR NAME], saved as a skill called team-audit. Its only job is to check changes to my team. Whenever I review, build, move or retire an agent, [AUDITOR NAME] checks:
1. Nothing missed: every existing scheduled task is accounted for
2. Nothing lost: the new agent does everything the old task did
3. Schedule right: correct day, time and time zone, and in the cloud if I asked for that
4. Safety: the agent saves drafts only and can't post, send, publish or delete
5. Team HQ is up to date
Return PASS or a numbered list of problems. I don't switch or retire anything until [AUDITOR NAME] says PASS. Add [AUDITOR NAME] to "The team" in Team HQ.

Stage 4 · Bring in the old hands

Turn the schedules you already have into team members

If you have never set up a scheduled task in Claude, skip to Stage 5. If you have, they are probably scattered, unnamed and half forgotten. Your chief of staff reviews what exists, the auditor checks the review, then you rebuild one job at a time, side by side with the old one, and only switch the old one off when the auditor says PASS.

Prompt 4.1 · the review

Your chief of staff reviews every schedule

[CHIEF OF STAFF NAME], review every scheduled task I currently have. For each one, tell me in a table: what it does, when it runs, what it reads, what it produces, and whether it's still useful.
Then turn the useful ones into team members who report to you. For each one, give me: its job, its day and time, where it reads from, what it produces, and the one thing it must never do. Suggest a name for each, and flag any that overlap so I can merge them.
Don't change, pause or delete any existing schedule. Wait for me to approve the team first.

Prompt 4.2 · the auditor checks the review

Check the review against what exists

[AUDITOR NAME], check [CHIEF OF STAFF NAME]'s review against the scheduled tasks that actually exist. Were any missed? Were any described wrongly? Is every "still useful" call backed by what the task has actually produced? Return PASS or the problems, and show me the corrected table.

Prompt 4.3 · you sign off

Approve the team

I approve this team, with these changes: [any names or jobs you want changed, or "none"]. Add every member to "The team" in Team HQ with their job, schedule and the old task they replace.

Prompt 4.4 · one at a time

Rebuild one old task as a team member

One job at a time. Repeat 4.4 to 4.6 for each.

Rebuild [OLD TASK NAME] as [AGENT NAME]: a skill that reads Team HQ first, runs team-quality-check, and saves its work as a draft for me to approve. Set it up as a cloud routine on the same schedule, in [your time zone], so it runs when my computer is off. If it needs files on my computer, which the cloud can't reach, tell me, and we'll decide whether to move them to Google Drive or keep it as a local scheduled task.
Run it once now and show me its result next to what the old task last produced. Don't pause the old task yet.

Prompt 4.5 · the auditor compares old and new

The side by side

[AUDITOR NAME], compare [AGENT NAME] with [OLD TASK NAME]. Tell me: does the new one do everything the old one did, is its schedule right, is it in the cloud, and is it drafts only? Return PASS or the problems.

The gateOnly move on when the auditor says PASS. If it lists problems, ask for those fixed and run 4.5 again.

Prompt 4.6 · pause, never delete

Retire the old task

[AUDITOR NAME] has passed [AGENT NAME]. Pause [OLD TASK NAME]. Don't delete it. Save a copy of its instructions in Team HQ under "Retired tasks", then confirm only one version of this job is scheduled.

Why pause and not delete. If the new agent turns out to be missing something in week three, the old instructions are sitting in Team HQ and the old task is one switch away.

Stage 5 · First day on the job

Hire a specialist for a job nobody is doing yet

Take the job you wrote down in Stage 1, the one you repeat every week by hand. This is the full hire, and every specialist after it uses the same five prompts. The interview is the point: you learn what an agent needs by answering its questions, so answer them yourself.

Prompt 5.1 · optional, if you are not sure what to hire

Plan the team

Ask me which jobs eat my time each week. Then, using my answers and what you've seen in my email, calendar and files, suggest up to four specialists to report to [CHIEF OF STAFF NAME], each owning one job I repeat every week.
For each one, give me one line each: its job, its day and time, where it reads from, what it produces, and the one thing it must never do. Put it in a table, then ask me to name each one.

Name them all, build one. The rest go on your org chart as the next hires.

Prompt 5.2 · the job interview

Interview-built specialist

[CHIEF OF STAFF NAME], let's hire a new team member for [THE JOB]. Interview me one question at a time:
1. What's the job, step by step?
2. Which day and time should it run?
3. Where does it read from, and where should it leave its work?
4. What does a great result look like? I'll show you an example
5. What must it never do?
Then write it as a skill called [AGENT NAME]. It must read Team HQ first, run team-quality-check, save its work as a draft for me to approve, and never post, send or publish anything.

Prompt 5.3 · skip if it doesn't write anything

Teach it your voice

Here are examples of how I write: [paste 5 to 10 real captions, emails or posts]. And here are lines that sound wrong for me, so never write like this: [paste 3 to 5 lines that sound like AI, or things I would never say]. Use this voice in everything [AGENT NAME] writes, and add both lists to "Voice" in Team HQ so the whole team uses them.

Paste what worked, not what you think you should write. The agent copies what you give it. The second list matters as much as the first: left alone, an agent drifts towards tidy, polished lines that nobody would mistake for you.

Prompt 5.4 · the trial shift

Run it for real

Run [AGENT NAME] now on real material, including team-quality-check. Show me the result in chat. Don't save anything yet.

Check

Voice

Does it sound like you?

Check

Facts

Are the details right?

Check

The job

Did it do all of what you asked?

Check

Safety

Did it stay a draft?

If one is off, fix that one thing and run it again. See Fix one thing below. Never start the recipe over.

Prompt 5.5 · hired

Audit it, then give it a shift

That's right. Save this as the process for [AGENT NAME]. [AUDITOR NAME], check it: drafts only, reads Team HQ, runs the quality check. If it passes, set it up as a cloud routine every [DAY] at [TIME] [your time zone], named "[AGENT NAME] · [THE JOB] · [DAY]", with only the connectors it needs. Add it to "The team" in Team HQ, and let me know when its first draft is ready.

Why the name and the connectors. Named the same way every time, your list of routines reads like a roster, and you can see at a glance who does what and when. And an agent that only needs your calendar should not be able to reach your email, so check which connectors each routine has and take off the ones it does not use.

Stage 6 · Roll call

Keep the team honest: weekly roll call, monthly performance review

A team that nobody checks on drifts. These three prompts take about ten minutes a week between them.

Prompt 6.1 · every Monday

Roll call

[CHIEF OF STAFF NAME], roll call. For every team member, tell me when it last ran, whether it worked, and what it left in Drafts. Flag anything that failed, didn't run, or ran twice.

Prompt 6.2 · whenever it gets something wrong

Fix one thing

[AGENT NAME] got this wrong: [WHAT WAS WRONG]. Fix just that part, save it as the process, and add the lesson to Team HQ so the whole team learns it.
  1. Name one thing. “The second paragraph is too stiff. Redo just that part.”
  2. Save the fix. “That's right. Save this as the process.”
  3. Tell HQ. “Add this to Team HQ so the whole team learns it.”

Never restart the recipe. Agents get better from specific corrections. Starting over throws away every fix you have already made.

Prompt 6.3 · once a month

The performance review

[AUDITOR NAME], review the whole team. For each member: is it still useful, is it drifting or repeating itself, does it overlap with another, and is it still drafts only? Recommend what to keep, change, merge or retire. Don't change anything until I approve.
