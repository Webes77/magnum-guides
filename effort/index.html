About this guide

This is a living guide. AI tools change often, so I update this page when the advice changes. Check the date at the top and the update log at the bottom to see what is current.

Version 1 is based on guidance from Anthropic's Claude Code team, published September 2026, covering Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1. In Claude Code you change effort with the /effort command. Other Claude apps may show it differently or not at all.

What effort is

Effort tells Claude how much work to put into a task before it hands back an answer. Low effort means work fast and give me a starting point. High effort means take your time and get it right on your own.

Effort does not make Claude smarter. It changes how much Claude checks its own work.

What changes when you turn it up

Higher effort buys you three things:

  • More checking. Claude tests its work, looks for problems and fixes them before it answers.
  • More edge cases caught. Claude finds the unusual situations that break a first draft.
  • More decisions made for you. Claude fills gaps in your brief with its own judgement.

The trade-off is time and usage. In Anthropic's tests, one task took 1 minute on low and 28 minutes on max.

One limit matters most. Higher effort fixes missed details. It does not fix a wrong approach. If your brief points Claude the wrong way, more effort takes it further the wrong way.

The levels and when to use each

Match the level to how much you want to stay involved and how costly a mistake would be.

LevelUse it whenBusiness example
LowYou want a fast first draft to react toBrainstorming offer names, sketching a page layout
MediumYou are doing everyday work with a clear briefBuilding a new template, drafting a process
HighMistakes are costly or there are lots of exceptionsChecking a pricing calculator, reviewing a contract summary, fixing something that already exists
MaxYou want Claude to work alone on something hardBuilding and testing a full tool from start to finish

Medium is the right default for most people most of the time.

A fifth setting

Claude Code also offers xhigh, which sits between High and Max. This guide works with the four above.

Your brief matters more than the setting

When Anthropic gave Claude a detailed brief, every effort level produced similar results. The vague brief was where the levels split apart.

This is why the 6 Lever Framework comes first. A brief with clear Role, Context, Constraints, Tone, Format and Output leaves Claude less to guess. Less guessing means a lower effort level gets you a good result, faster.

If your results are poor, fix the brief before you raise the effort.

The working loop

Use this loop for anything you are building with Claude.

  1. Give Claude your brief and ask it to interview you about anything missing.
  2. Answer its questions.
  3. Build on low effort.
  4. Check that it got the main idea right.
  5. Adjust on low effort until the direction is right.
  6. Switch to high effort and ask Claude to test and check the finished work.

You stay in control while the direction is being set. Claude does the heavy checking once the direction is locked.

Quick check before you start

Ask yourself two questions.

  • Do I want to stay involved? Yes: go low or medium. No: go high or max.
  • Would a mistake cost me money, time or trust? Yes: go high, at least for the final check.
