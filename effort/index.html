Effort doesn't make Claude smarter

It decides how hard Claude checks its own work, and how often it stops to ask you. Here's which level to use, and when.

Updated 29 Sep 2026

What effort is

Effort is how much work you ask Claude to do before it hands something back.

Think of briefing a tradie. Low effort is "give me a rough price on the back of a napkin". High effort is "come out, measure up, and don't call me until it's right".

Same tradie. Same skills. What changes is how much checking happens before you see anything.

That's the bit people miss. Effort doesn't make Claude smarter. It makes Claude check its own work.

In Claude Code you set it by typing /effort. Other Claude apps may show it differently, or not at all.

What turning it up buys you

Turn it up and you get three things.

  • More checking. Claude tests its own work, finds the problems and fixes them before you see them.
  • More odd cases caught. The strange situation that breaks a first draft gets found first.
  • More decisions made for you. Wherever your brief has a gap, Claude fills it with its own judgement.

The catch is time, and usage. In Anthropic's tests the same job took 1 minute on low and 28 minutes on max. That's a coffee against a long lunch.

1 min on low · 28 min on max

And one limit matters more than the rest. More effort fixes missed details. It doesn't fix the wrong direction. Put the wrong address in the satnav and driving more carefully won't get you home. If your brief points Claude the wrong way, max effort just takes it further down the wrong road.

Which level, when

Two things decide it. How involved you want to stay, and what a mistake would cost you.

LevelUse it whenBusiness example
LowYou want a quick rough draft to react toBrainstorming names for an offer, sketching a page layout
MediumEveryday work, and you've given a clear briefBuilding a new template, writing up a process
HighA mistake would cost you, or there are lots of exceptionsChecking a pricing calculator, reviewing a contract summary, fixing something that already exists
MaxYou want Claude to go away and do the hard thing aloneBuilding and testing a whole tool, start to finish

Medium is the right place to sit for most people, most of the time. Leave it there unless you've got a reason not to.

A fifth setting

Claude Code also has xhigh, which sits between High and Max. This guide sticks to the four above.

The brief beats the setting

This was the finding that stood out. When Anthropic gave Claude a detailed brief, every effort level came back with much the same result. The vague brief was where the levels split apart.

Which makes sense. Give a new staff member a proper handover and they'll do a good job whether they rush it or not. Give them two lines and "you'll work it out", and suddenly how hard they try matters a lot.

That's why the brief comes first. The six-line checklist is one quick way to write it: Role, Context, Constraints, Tone, Format, Output. Get those clear and Claude has less to guess. Less guessing means a lower setting does the job, and does it faster.

So if the results are poor, fix the brief before you touch the effort.

The working loop

Use this for anything you're building with Claude.

  1. Give Claude your brief and ask it to interview you about anything missing.
  2. Answer its questions.
  3. Build it on low.
  4. Check it got the main idea right.
  5. Keep adjusting on low until the direction is right.
  6. Switch to high and ask Claude to test and check the finished work.

Cheap while you're still changing your mind. Thorough once you're not. You stay in charge of where it's going, and Claude does the heavy checking at the end.

Quick check before you start

Two questions.

  • Do I want to stay in the loop? Yes: low or medium. No: high or max.
  • Would a mistake cost me money, time or trust? Yes: go high, at least for the final check.
