Which AI tools are worth paying for?

What each tool does, whether I’d pay for it, and what you probably already have. Three questions below give you your own short list.

Prices checked 11 October 2026. They are what each company lists, mostly in US dollars, with GST added at checkout on an Australian card. They move often, so check on the day.

The short version

Check the shed before you buy a drill

Most small businesses I work with already pay for half the AI they need. They just haven’t found it yet. Gemini comes with most Google Workspace plans. Copilot Chat comes with Microsoft 365. Claude’s slides come with every Claude plan, even the free one.

So start with what you’ve got. Then add one tool per job, and only once that job keeps coming back. Three tools doing the same thing is like paying for three streaming services and watching one.

I use most of what’s on this page. Claude every day, plus ChatGPT, Gemini, Gamma, Manus, Wispr Flow and Fireflies. Where I haven’t used something, I say so.

Your main AI 4 tools

Pay for one. Pick it by where your business already works, and use the free versions of the others beside it.

Claude

Worth it

Writes, thinks and works through your documents with you. It keeps your standing instructions and files in Projects, so you’re not explaining the business from scratch every time.

It’s what I use every day, and what I teach. It’s the best of the three at writing that sounds like a person, and at long documents. Since 6 October it works inside Google Docs, Sheets and Slides on paid plans, and it connects to Microsoft 365 too. What it doesn’t do is make photos or video, so you’ll want something beside it for that.

Price
Free plan for light use. Pro is A$34 a month including GST, billed in Australian dollars.
Watch for
The free plan’s limit resets every five hours. On a real working day you’ll hit it.

ChatGPT

Only if

The all-rounder. Writing, pictures, voice and research in one app.

I use it beside Claude, mostly for pictures. It’s the pick if you’ll only pay for one tool and you need images as well as words. Since 8 October the free version runs on GPT-6, so if you only need it now and then, don’t pay for it.

Price
Free, with ads. Go is about A$13 a month, also with ads. Plus is US$20 a month, which comes to roughly A$30 to A$35 here.
Watch for
It makes no video now. OpenAI closed Sora in April.

Gemini

Only if

Google’s assistant, built into Gmail, Docs, Sheets and Meet, with the best picture and video making of the three.

I use it for pictures and video, where it’s ahead of Claude and ChatGPT. If your business runs on Google Workspace Business Standard or higher, it’s already in your plan. Switch it on before you buy anything else. If you’re not on Workspace, Google AI Plus is the cheapest real paid AI plan going.

Price
Free. Google AI Plus is A$7.99 a month. Google AI Pro is about A$33 a month. Included in Workspace Business Standard and up.
Watch for
Workspace Business Starter gets a thin version. Free accounts dropped to Google’s lightest model on 9 October.

Copilot

Only ifFrom research, not use

Microsoft’s assistant. Copilot Chat comes free with Microsoft 365 business plans.

I don’t use it, so this one comes from research rather than experience. If you’re on Microsoft 365, use the free Copilot Chat first. The paid version puts it inside Outlook, Word, Excel and Teams, and costs about as much per person as a whole Claude Pro seat. Claude’s Microsoft 365 connector covers a lot of the same ground.

Price
Copilot Chat is included. Copilot Business is AU$31.40 a person a month on a yearly plan, plus GST.
Watch for
Microsoft changes how Copilot is licensed often. It changed twice this year.
Presentations 3 tools

Claude Slides

Worth it

Claude builds an editable deck from your notes, inside the chat. You can present it from Claude or download it as PowerPoint or PDF.

It’s free on every Claude plan since 8 October, so it’s where I’d start. It’s already there, and if you’ve set up a Project it already knows your business.

Price
Included in every Claude plan, the free one too.
Watch for
It’s weeks old. How cleanly the PowerPoint file edits afterwards isn’t documented yet, so check before you send one to a client.

Gamma

Worth it

Type or paste what you want to say and it builds a designed deck, document or one-page website.

One of my favourites. It’s the quickest route I know from rough notes to something that looks designed, and it connects to Claude, so Claude can brief it for you. Gamma 5 rebuilt the product on 6 October, so any how-to video older than that is out of date.

Price
Free to try, on a one-off batch of credits. Plus is US$9 a month.
Watch for
Export to PowerPoint is the weak spot. If a client needs to edit your file, open it yourself first.

Canva

Only if

Canva’s AI drafts a deck from a prompt, in your brand kit, and makes the odd picture and short clip too.

I don’t use it much. But if you already pay for Canva Pro and your brand kit is set up, use it before adding Gamma. It’s not worth buying Canva for presentations alone.

Price
Canva Pro is about A$20 to A$22 a month, and Canva’s Australian prices include GST.
Watch for
All of Canva’s AI tools draw on one shared monthly allowance.
Talking instead of typing 2 tools

You say more when you talk than when you type, and the AI does better with more to go on. The microphone on your phone keyboard is fine for a short message. For real writing at a desk, one of these.

Wispr Flow

Worth it

Hold a key, talk, and clean text appears wherever your cursor is. The ums and false starts come out.

I use it every day. Start on the free plan. If you hit the weekly limit, that’s your answer: you’re using it enough to pay for it.

Price
Free for 2,000 words a week on Mac and Windows. Pro is US$12 a month on annual billing, US$15 monthly.
Watch for
It works in the cloud. Turn Privacy Mode on, because it’s off by default.

Fireflies Talk

Worth it

The same hold-a-key dictation, inside the Fireflies desktop app.

New on 29 September, and free on every Fireflies plan with no word limit. It’s too new for me to rank above Wispr Flow, but if you’re on Fireflies already, try it before paying for anything.

Price
Free. Mac and Windows now, phone apps to come.
Watch for
Nobody has tested its accuracy independently yet.
Meeting notes 3 options

What you already have

Check first

Your meeting platform may already take notes for you.

  • Google Meet. Gemini takes notes on Workspace Business Standard and higher, not Starter. Someone has to switch it on in the admin settings, and in a small business that’s usually you.
  • Zoom. Meeting summaries are included on paid Zoom plans.
  • Teams. The meeting recap needs Teams Premium or a Copilot licence on top of Microsoft 365. Buying Copilot just for notes costs more than Fireflies.

The catch is that each one only works on its own platform. If your calls run across all three, that’s when Fireflies earns its place.

Fireflies

Worth it

Joins your Zoom, Meet or Teams call, or records from your computer, and gives you a transcript, summary and action items you can search later.

It’s what I use. It works on every platform, and the notes reach Claude through the official connector, so Claude can read last week’s call before it writes the proposal.

Price
Free to trial. Pro is US$10 a month on annual billing, US$18 monthly.
Watch for
The bot shows up on the call as “Notetaker”. Some clients don’t like it, so tell them before the call.

Granola

Skip for nowFrom research, not use

A notepad on your computer that listens to the call with no bot, then turns your rough notes into clean ones.

I haven’t used it, and plenty of people rate it. I’m leaving it off for now. A US class action filed in July claims it records people without their consent and trains on their data by default. Those are claims, not a ruling. But Fireflies does the same job, so there’s no reason to carry the risk while it plays out.

Price
Free to try. Business is US$14 a person a month.
Tell people the call is recorded

Every time, whatever tool. In Queensland you can record a call you’re part of without asking, but other states have stricter rules, and a call across states should follow the stricter one. A tool with no bot on screen makes this your job entirely. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

Pictures 4 tools

ChatGPT Images

Worth it

Makes and edits pictures by asking for them inside ChatGPT.

My pick when the picture has words on it: a menu, a sale tile, a sign. It’s the best of these at getting text right. If you pay for ChatGPT Plus, it costs nothing extra.

Price
Inside ChatGPT. The free plan gets a few a day, and OpenAI doesn’t publish the limits.

Gemini

Worth it

Google’s picture maker, called Nano Banana, inside the Gemini app.

I use it for realistic photos, and it’s the best value of the lot. A new version arrived on 6 October that’s better at text than the last one.

Price
Google AI Plus is A$7.99 a month. Google AI Pro, about A$33, adds video.

Ideogram

Only if

A picture maker built around getting the words right: posters, banners, signs.

I use it for text-heavy graphics. The version that landed on 30 September lets you change the price or the date on a graphic without the rest of it shifting. Worth it if that’s a weekly job. Otherwise ChatGPT covers it.

Price
Free to try. Plus is from US$15 a month on annual billing.
Watch for
Pictures made on the free plan are public by default.

Fal.ai

Skip

A pay-as-you-go shop for hundreds of image and video models, charged one picture or one second at a time.

I use it, because I like testing new models the week they land. You almost certainly don’t need it. It’s built for developers, and the plan you already have does the job with far less to learn.

Price
Pay per result. There’s no monthly plan.
Never fake the real thing

A made-up photo of a job you didn’t do, a dish you don’t serve or stock you don’t carry can break Australian consumer law. The law asks whether the picture misleads, not who made it. Pictures from Gemini and ChatGPT carry an invisible mark, and since 7 October anyone can check for it.

Short videos 2 tools

Gemini

Worth it

Short clips with sound, from a sentence or a photo, in the Gemini app or Google’s Flow.

Google’s models are what I use for video. On Google AI Pro you get pictures and video on one bill, which is the simplest setup I know. Clips run about eight to ten seconds, which suits a Reel or a story, not an explainer.

Price
Included in Google AI Pro, about A$33 a month. Google AI Plus gets a small allowance, around ten good clips a month.
Watch for
The video model in the Gemini app is now called Omni. In Flow it’s still Veo. Same job, two names.

Higgsfield

Only if

One subscription that reaches many video models, Seedance and Kling among them, plus ready-made styles and ad tools.

It’s what I publish with, and Seedance is my pick inside it for longer, consistent clips. It has a Claude connector, too. But it’s only worth it if video or ads are a weekly job. There are billing complaints and renewals aren’t refunded, so pay monthly until you know you’ll use it.

Price
Plans start somewhere between US$15 and US$19 a month. Sources disagree, so check the page.
Watch for
Never put a real person or a famous character in a clip without permission.
Social posts 2 tools

Buffer

Worth it

One queue for all your social accounts. You or Claude put posts in, and Buffer publishes them at the times you set.

It’s what I teach in Social Media with AI. Claude drafts straight into it, and you check each post on your phone before it goes out.

Price
Free for three channels. Essentials is US$6 a channel a month, or US$5 on annual billing.
Watch for
Claude connects through Buffer’s own connector, which you add by hand rather than from Claude’s list.

Meta Business Suite

Only if

Meta’s own scheduler for Facebook and Instagram.

If those two are all you post to, use this. It’s free. The moment you add LinkedIn, Google or TikTok, or want Claude filling the queue, move to Buffer.

Price
Free.
Automation 3 tools
Check Claude first

If the job runs on a timetable and ends in a draft you check, like a Monday summary of last week’s enquiries, a Claude scheduled task may do it with no other tool. If it has to fire the moment a form comes in, that’s a job for one of these.

Zapier

Worth it

Connects your apps, so when something happens in one, something happens in another without you.

The one to start with. It’s the easiest to learn on your own, and it builds your first automation from a sentence. I use it alongside Make.

Price
Free for 100 tasks a month, two-step jobs only. Professional is from US$19.99 a month on annual billing.
Watch for
Any job with an AI step in the middle needs Professional.

Make

Only if

The same job as Zapier, on a visual canvas where you can see every step.

I use it for bigger builds, because it’s far cheaper per step. It’s also harder to learn on your own. Move to it when a Zapier bill has grown past what the job is worth, or when someone who knows Make is building it for you.

Price
Free for 1,000 credits a month. Core is from about US$9 a month on annual billing, US$12 monthly.
Watch for
A job that checks a folder every 15 minutes uses about 2,880 credits a month even when nothing’s there. Set it to hourly.

n8n

SkipFrom research, not use

Another app connector, popular with technical people because it can run on your own server.

I don’t use it, though I hear good things. It’s built for someone technical who’ll own it. There’s no free plan unless you run your own server, and keeping that server patched is a security job. A serious flaw in it was being actively exploited in March.

Price
The hosted plan is from about €20 a month on annual billing.
A whole job 1 tool

Manus

Only if

An agent you hand a whole job to: research a market, build a page, make a deck. It works on its own computer in the cloud until it’s done.

One of my favourites. Think of it as a contractor, not an assistant. Give it the job you’d normally lose an afternoon to. Start on the free plan and only pay once a job has proved itself. There are two manuals for it in the members area: Manus Bridge and the website manual.

Price
Free daily credits. Pro is from US$20 a month.
Watch for
Credits make the bill hard to predict, and a job that goes wrong still spends them. In August Manus deleted some users’ data after a deal with Meta fell through. Keep a copy of anything important outside it.
