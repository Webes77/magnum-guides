What I’d hang on the wall, what to skip, and three questions to fill in your own.

Prices checked 11 to 14 October 2026

Before the list

A builder sits down at the end of the financial year with his card statement and a highlighter. He’s looking for fuel receipts. What he finds is AI.

ChatGPT. Claude. A video app he signed up to for one ad in March, still billing in October. A notetaker that joins calls he stopped having. A design tool someone set up for a flyer. About A$140 a month, and most weeks he opens two of them.

None of them is a bad tool. He bought them the way you fill the back of a ute: one at a time, each for a good reason, never all laid out at once.

Three tape measures, two hammers, no level.

I try most new tools the week they land, so my own statement isn’t one to wave around either. That’s why this page starts with the job, not the tool. One tool per job, the ones I’d hang on the wall, and the ones to rent for a month and hand back. And before any of it, check what you already pay for. Most small businesses already own half the AI they need.

Tool tip

Keep your regulars. Rent the rest.

AI models are going the way of streaming services. Most of what’s on them overlaps, and each one has a few things it does better than the rest. So you keep one or two as your regulars.

And when a show you want is only on one service, you sign up for a month, watch it, and cancel. Do the same with AI. A run of videos for a launch? Take a video tool for a month, then cancel. That’s why the cards below keep saying pay monthly.

Never fake the real thing

A made-up photo of a job you didn’t do, a dish you don’t serve or stock you don’t carry can break Australian consumer law. The law asks whether the picture misleads, not who made it. Pictures from Gemini and ChatGPT carry an invisible mark, and since 7 October anyone can check for it.
