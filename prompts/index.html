The method in five lines

  1. Give it a role. One sentence on who it is being sharpens everything after it.
  2. Say what you want, plainly, and why. It follows instructions literally now, and a reason lets it handle the case you did not name.
  3. Tell it what to do, not what to avoid. A rule with its reason beats a list of don’ts.
  4. Tell it when to ask and when to stop. If it might not know, it should say so rather than guess. Give it a finish line so it does not pad.
  5. Show it an example when the shape matters. One good example beats a paragraph of description.

Those five are the six levers in working order: Role, Context, Constraints, Tone, Format, Output. Every card below is built on them. Session one teaches them properly.
