Cowork, set up properly

A chat answers you. Cowork does the job. It is the same Claude you already pay for, in a mode where you hand over a task and get back the finished thing instead of a reply. This page is what it is, which one to open, the setup in six steps, and five prompts to run the day it is on.

The short version

Everything below, on one screen

Set it up once, about fifteen minutes

  1. Open the desktop app. If the message box still offers Chat and Cowork, pick Cowork.
  2. Give it one folder. Start with one, not your whole drive.
  3. Set it to check with you every time. Loosen it later.
  4. Connect Gmail, Drive and Calendar once, in Settings.
  5. Turn on skill creation, in Settings.
  6. Schedule a job once you have one worth repeating.

Then run these

  1. Clear the desktop
  2. Sort a folder by what is inside the files
  3. Turn a folder of documents into one spreadsheet
  4. Set a job to run every week without you
  5. Learn how I write, from my sent mail

That is the whole guide. Everything under it is the same thing with the reasons attached, for when you want to know why a step is there.

Anthropic is merging the two. Over the next few weeks the choice between Chat and Cowork disappears from the message box, and Claude works out for itself whether you have asked a question or handed over a job. What Cowork is

The part of Claude that does the work, not the talking

There is nothing to buy. Cowork comes with Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise, and if you are reading this you are on one of them. If the question is which plan your staff should be on rather than whether you have Cowork, that is a separate guide.

The difference between a chat and Cowork is the difference between asking someone a question and giving them a job. In a chat you send a message and read the answer, then send the next one. You are doing the driving. In Cowork you describe the outcome you want, Claude works out the steps, then goes and does them: reading your files, writing new ones, running code, looking things up. It comes back when the work is finished.

You describe the end

Not the steps. “Sort out my desktop and tell me what you moved” is the whole instruction. Working out the order is its job, not yours.

It works on its own

Many steps, one after another, without you approving each one. You set how often it stops to check with you, and that setting is yours to tighten.

You get the thing

A tidied folder, a spreadsheet, a written report, a file on your disk. Not a description of how you might do it.

The one-line versionA chat gives you an answer. Cowork gives you the work.

How it differs from a chat

Four differences that change what you can ask for

In a standard chatIn Cowork
One message, one answer. You send the next instruction yourself.One instruction, then as many steps as the job takes, until it is done.
It reads a file you upload or paste into the message box.It works inside a folder you have given it: reading, editing, creating and moving files in place.
It stops when you close the window.It keeps going after you close it, and a scheduled task runs with your computer switched off.
Nothing to set up. Open it and type.A folder and your permission settings, done once, then left alone.

What carries across, and what does not

This is the part people get wrong in both directions, so it is worth being exact.

Memory carries. Since 25 August 2026, what Claude remembers about you and your business is one store shared by chat and Cowork. Something you established in a chat is there in a Cowork task, and something that came up in a Cowork task is there next time you chat. You can read the lot under Settings › Memory, where it is listed by topic, and delete anything you would rather it forgot.

The conversation does not. A new Cowork task does not go and read yesterday's chat thread. So anything a task needs in order to be done properly has to be in the task itself, in the folder you gave it, or in your instructions. That is why the setup below is worth the fifteen minutes.

Desktop or web

Open the desktop app

That is the answer for almost everybody, for one reason: your files are on your computer, and the desktop app is what reaches them.

Cowork runs on desktop for Mac and Windows, on the web, and on the phone app. The work itself now happens on Anthropic's servers rather than on your machine, which is why a task keeps running after you close the lid and why a session you start at your desk is there on your phone. What the desktop app adds is the bridge to your own files and the built-in browser.

Web and mobile have been rolling out since 7 July 2026, starting with Max and working down the plans, and they are still in beta. Use them the way you would use a phone: to check on a task, read what came back, or start something while you are away from your desk. Do the setup on the desktop.

Setting it up

Six steps, about fifteen minutes, done once

  1. Open Cowork

    Open the Claude desktop app and sign in. If the message box still offers a choice between Chat and Cowork, pick Cowork. If it does not, your account is already on the merged version and there is nothing to switch.

    Either way you are in the right place when you can describe a job rather than ask a question.

  2. Give it one folder

    Cowork does not create a folder for itself and there is no default location. You choose an existing folder and grant access to it, either in the settings or when Claude asks for it partway through a job.

    Start with one folder, not your whole drive. A project folder, or Desktop if the first thing you want is the desktop cleared. You can add more later, and the smaller the first grant, the easier it is to see what it did.

    Worth knowingGranting a folder is not the same as uploading a file. Nothing is copied anywhere. Claude reads and changes the files where they already sit, including moving and renaming them, which is the whole point and also the reason the prompts below never delete anything. The wording on the access button is the one thing on this page that does not come from Anthropic's own documentation, so it may read slightly differently on your screen.

  3. Set how often it checks with you

    Cowork asks before it uses a connected tool, and you control how often. The choice is between asking you every time, asking once and then carrying on, or not asking at all.

    Start on the setting that asks every time. Once you have watched it work through two or three jobs and you trust what it reaches for, loosen it. Going the other way, tightening after something surprising, is a worse way to learn.

  4. Connect Gmail, Drive and Calendar once

    Settings › Connectors

    Connectors are not a separate Cowork setting. You authorise Gmail, Google Drive or Calendar once, in the same place you would for chat, and they are available in both. If you already connected them for your everyday chats, they are already on here and there is nothing to do at this step.

  5. Turn on skill creation

    Settings › Capabilities › Skills

    A skill is a job description you write once and Claude loads whenever it applies. Switch on the skill creator, and from then on you can describe a skill in an ordinary conversation and have Claude write it for you. The last prompt below ends by producing one.

  6. Schedule something, when you have a job worth repeating

    Type /schedule inside a Cowork task, or open Scheduled in the left sidebar to see and manage the ones you have.

    Scheduled tasks run on Anthropic's servers, so your computer does not need to be awake or even on. Leave this one until you have a job you have run by hand a few times and want repeated. A schedule is a good way to automate a job you have already got right, and a fast way to repeat one you have not.

Five prompts to run first

Four of these are jobs a standard chat cannot do at all

A chat can tell you how to tidy a folder. It cannot open the folder. The first four below need Cowork because they read and change files that live on your computer, or they keep running when you have shut the lid. The fifth is the one that pays you back every day afterwards.

All five are on the Prompt Shelf, so you can find them again without coming back here. Paste each into a new Cowork task. Where a prompt has words in square brackets, replace them with your own.

Prompt one · a Cowork task, with your Desktop folder granted

Clear the desktop

The first job worth giving it. The result is visible in ten minutes, and it teaches you what folder access really means. It never deletes: everything moves to one dated review folder with a written record of every move.

Read it first 
You are a careful assistant sorting out my computer desktop. Nothing on it
is yours to throw away, so this job is to sort and move, never to delete.

Work only in the folder I have given you access to.

The rules, and why each one is there:
- Move files, never delete them. I am the only one who knows which of these
  still matter, so the deciding is mine to do.
- Put everything you move into one folder named Review, followed by today's
  date, so nothing is lost while I work through it.
- Leave anything you cannot identify exactly where it is, and list it for
  me. A guess about where a file belongs is worse than leaving it alone.
- Check with me before moving anything that looks like it is in use: a file
  changed today, or a lock file.

Work through this in order:
1. List what is on the desktop. Group it by what it is: documents,
   images, screenshots, installers, folders, and anything else.
2. Show me the groups, tell me how many files are in each, and say where
   you propose to put each group. Then wait for me to say go.
3. Once I say go, create the folders and move the files.
4. Write a file named what-moved.md inside the review folder, listing every
   file, where it came from and where it went, so I can undo any single
   move by hand.

Stop after step 4. Tell me what you left behind and why.

After it runs. Open what-moved.md first. It is the list of every move, and it is what makes each one reversible.

Prompt two · a Cowork task, with the messy folder granted

Sort a folder by what is inside the files

The one to run if you only run one. Every filing job you have put off is the same problem: the names are wrong, so sorting the folder means opening every file. Cowork opens every file.

Read it first 
You are a careful assistant filing documents by what is actually inside
them, not by what they are called. The names in a folder like this are
usually wrong, which is the reason the filing never got done.

Work only in the folder I have given you access to. Move files, never
delete them, because I am the only one who can decide what goes.

Open each file and read enough of it to tell what it is and who it
concerns. Do not judge a file by its name or its extension.

Work through this in order:
1. Read every file in the folder, including the ones in subfolders.
2. Propose a filing structure based on what you actually found, rather
   than what you think I expect. Show me the folder names and how many
   files go in each.
3. List anything that does not fit, instead of forcing it into the
   nearest folder. A misfiled document is harder to find than a loose one.
4. Wait for me to say go.
5. Once I say go, create the folders and move the files. Where a file name
   tells me nothing, rename it as YYYY-MM-DD-who-what, taking the date
   from inside the document rather than from the file.
6. Write a file named filing-record.md listing every file, its old name
   and place, and its new ones.

Stop after step 6. Tell me how many files you could not identify.

After it runs. Read the structure it proposes before you say go. It filed on what it found, so the folders may not be the ones you expected.

Prompt three · a Cowork task, with the folder of documents granted

Turn a folder of documents into one spreadsheet

A year of receipts as PDFs and photos. In a chat you would upload them one at a time and retype the numbers. Here you point at the folder once. It never calculates a figure it could not read.

Read it first 
You are a bookkeeper's assistant turning a pile of documents into one
spreadsheet I can sort and total.

Work in the folder I have given you access to. Read every document in it,
including scans and photographs.

Pull these fields out of each one:
[List the columns you want. For example: date, supplier, description,
amount excluding GST, GST, total, invoice number.]

The rules, and why each one is there:
- Take every figure from the document itself. Do not calculate a missing
  total or read a date off the file name, because a number I did not
  check is worse to me than a blank one.
- Where a field is missing or you cannot read it, write NOT FOUND in that
  cell and carry on.
- Where a document is not the kind of thing I am asking about, leave it
  out and list it separately rather than forcing a row out of it.

Work through this in order:
1. Read every document in the folder.
2. Build a spreadsheet with one row per document and one column per
   field, sorted oldest first.
3. Add a last row totalling the number columns.
4. Save it into the same folder as a spreadsheet file.
5. Tell me how many documents you read, how many cells say NOT FOUND,
   and which files you left out and why.

Stop after step 5.

After it runs. Search the sheet for NOT FOUND. Those are the only cells needing your eyes.

Prompt four · a Cowork task, once you have a job you have run by hand

Set a job to run every week without you

The one no chat can touch. A scheduled task runs on Anthropic's servers, so it happens on Monday at seven whether your Mac is open, asleep or in a bag.

Read it first 
You are setting up a job that will run on a schedule while I am not
watching, so it has to be written to fail safely rather than to guess.

The job:
[Say what you want done, and how you want the result delivered. For
example: check the shared folder for new enquiries and write a summary of
anything that arrived since last week.]

Before you schedule anything:
1. Run the job once now, while I am here, and show me the result.
2. Tell me what you would do if something is missing or unreadable on a
   week when I am not around to ask.
3. Wait for me to say go.

Once I say go:
4. Set the job to run [say when, for example every Monday at 7am].
5. Write each result into the folder as its own file with the date in the
   name, rather than replacing the last one, so I can see what changed.
6. When there is nothing to report, write that plainly rather than
   writing nothing, because an empty folder and a broken job look the
   same from the outside.

Stop after step 6. Tell me where the results will appear.

After it runs. Watch the first real run land. A scheduled job you have never seen produce a file is one you are only assuming works.

Prompt five · a Cowork task, with Gmail connected

Learn how I write, from my sent mail

The odd one out. A chat with Gmail connected could read your sent mail too, so this is not a demonstration of Cowork. It is what makes everything you write afterwards sound like you.

Read it first 
You are a writing analyst working out how I actually write, so that Claude
can draft in my voice instead of its own.

Read my sent mail only. What I received is other people's writing, and it
would teach you the wrong voice.

Count an email as mine when I wrote it from scratch, it runs to more than
about eighty words, and it went to a real person. Skip forwards, one-line
replies, automated mail, and anything I pasted in from somewhere else,
because none of it shows how I write.

Work through this in order:
1. Read at least thirty of my sent emails from the last twelve months.
2. Tell me what you found, under these headings:
   - Sentence length and rhythm, with two real examples.
   - Words and phrases I use often, and ones I never use.
   - How I open, and how I sign off.
   - How formal I am, and whether that changes with who I am writing to.
   - What I do that most people do not.
3. Mark any point you are drawing from fewer than five emails as
   [NEEDS CONFIRMING], so I can tell a real pattern from a one-off.
4. Ask me which of it to keep, and which is a habit I would rather drop.
   Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer.
5. Write the result as a skill: a description saying when it should fire,
   then the rules for writing as me, each rule with its reason.

Stop after step 5 and show me the skill. I will save it myself.

After it runs. Anything marked [NEEDS CONFIRMING] came from too few examples to trust. Confirm it or cut it.
