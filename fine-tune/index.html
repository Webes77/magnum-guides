Your AI is guessing at who you are, and it's costing you every single day. Every flat answer, every time you re-explain your business, every draft that sounds like it was written for someone else. None of that is the AI being average. It's the AI working off a bad brief, or no brief at all. This guide fixes the brief.

Before anything else, here's the furniture, because the rest of this document assumes you know it.

Your account instructions. One box, buried in your settings, that the AI reads before every single message you send. Whatever's in there shapes every answer you ever get. Most are empty or ancient.

Your projects. Separate workspaces you set up for one job each, a client, an offer, a type of work. Each one has its own instructions and its own files, so the AI can be a specialist there instead of a generalist everywhere.

Files. Documents you attach so the AI actually knows things, your pricing, your process, your standards, instead of guessing at them. Instructions tell it how to behave. Files tell it what to know.

Cowork. The version of the AI that goes off and does real work, files, tasks, multi-step jobs, rather than just chatting. It has its own separate setup, and tuning your chats does not tune this.

That's it. That's the whole machine. And "optimising" just means making sure each of those four things is current, clean, and not fighting the others. No code, no technical skill. You paste in a prompt, read what comes back, and keep what you're happy with.

The whole pass takes an afternoon if you do everything, and most people shouldn't. Part One alone takes ten minutes and you'll feel the difference the same day.

One rule runs under all of it: audit first, approve second, rewrite third, implement last. The AI never changes anything on its own. You read everything before it goes in. You stay in charge the whole way through.

YOUR ACCOUNT INSTRUCTIONS

The setup that sits under everything. Fix this first, because every project runs on top of it.

THE POINTOne box in your settings shapes every answer you ever get.
WHAT IT'S WORTHYou stop re-explaining yourself forever. Sharper answers from the next chat you open.
WHAT MOST PEOPLE GET WRONGThey think the AI gets to know them the more they use it. It doesn't. It reads this box. Empty box, stranger every time.
TIMETen minutes.
WHICH ONE IS YOU? Box empty or never touched? Start at 1A. Got something in there already? Start at 1B. Everyone does 1C.
STEP 1A

Your AI has no idea who you are, and it's costing you every single day.

  • The AI reads one box before every message you send. Yours is empty.
  • One prompt: it drafts your instructions from what it already knows about you.
  • You read it, fix it, paste it in. Two minutes.

Most people never fill this in. You open ChatGPT or Claude, you start typing, and you assume it just knows how to help you. It doesn't. Out of the box, it's built for everyone, which means it's built for no one. It writes the way it thinks the average person wants, guesses at the detail you need, and has no clue what you actually do for a living.

So you spend your life re-explaining yourself. Every new chat, you're setting the scene again: who you are, what your business does, how you like things written, what to leave out. You've probably typed the same background paragraph a hundred times without realising it. That's not the AI being difficult. That's you doing its onboarding by hand, every time, forever.

There's a box that fixes this permanently. It's called your custom instructions, and right now yours is empty. It's the one place the AI reads before every message you ever send. Fill it in properly once, and it carries into every chat from then on, no reminding, no repeating.

Here's what happens when you do.

You run one prompt. The AI takes everything it already knows about you from how you've been talking to it, and drafts your instructions for you. You don't fill in a form. You don't answer twenty questions. It writes the first version, you read it, you fix anything that's off, you paste it in. Two minutes.

From that moment, it knows you. It knows your business, how you like things done, what good looks like. Every answer comes back sharper and more like you, straight away. You stop re-explaining. You stop starting from scratch.

This is the foundation everything else sits on, so if your box is empty, this is where you start.

WHERE TO RUN THIS

A brand-new chat, not inside a project. Works in ChatGPT or Claude.

Here's how.

THE PROMPT · PASTE THIS IN
My account custom instructions are empty and I want you to write them for me.

Do not send me a list of questions. Use what you already know about me from how we've talked and anything you remember. Build the draft from that. If you genuinely don't have enough to go on, ask me three quick questions, no more, then build it.

Keep it 150 to 400 words. Include only what belongs at this level: how I communicate, how much detail I want, how I like decisions made, the standards I hold you to, and the mistakes to avoid. Do not include client names, pricing, research, or anything that changes often. That lives in files later, not here.

Answer in this exact format. Nothing else.

YOUR DRAFT INSTRUCTIONS: (the full set in a copy-paste block, 150 to 400 words. This is a draft for you to approve. I have not saved anything. Paste it in yourself only if you're happy.)
WHAT I ASSUMED: (max 4 bullets, one line each. The guesses I made about you, so you can correct anything wrong before you use it.)

Keep it short. No preamble, no summary. Stop after the assumptions.

If the box won't take the full draft, your plan has a smaller character limit. Ask it to cut the draft to 250 words and paste that instead. Free ChatGPT accounts cap the box at roughly 250 words; paid plans and Claude take the full version.

You'll know it worked when the draft sounds like something you'd actually say about yourself, not a LinkedIn bio.
BY HAND
  1. Copy the draft it gave you.
  2. On ChatGPT: Settings, then Personalisation, then Custom Instructions. Paste it in and save.
  3. On Claude: Settings, then Profile. Paste it into the preferences box and save.
  4. Open a brand-new chat and ask it something you'd normally ask. It should already sound more like you.
STEP 1B

Right now, your AI is guessing at who you are.

  • Your old setup steers every answer you get, and it's quietly rotted.
  • The AI audits its own instructions and hands you a clean rewrite.
  • Paste the rewrite. Sharpest two minutes you'll spend on AI this year.

Think about how you set it up. You wrote some instructions once, maybe when you first got the account, maybe you copied something off a video. Then you never looked at them again. But here's the part nobody tells you: every single message you send, the AI reads that old setup first, before it even looks at your actual question. So whatever you dumped in there months ago is still steering every answer you get today.

And that setup has quietly rotted. It's too long, so the AI skims it. Half of it's out of date, because your business has moved on since you wrote it. Some of the rules contradict each other, so the AI picks one at random. And you've probably got stuff crammed in there that was never meant to live there in the first place, client names, pricing, notes to self, so the AI's wading through a filing cabinet every time it should just be answering you.

You feel it, even if you can't name it. The answers come back generic, like it's talking to a stranger. And you find yourself re-explaining the same context over and over. You've half-decided the AI just isn't that good. It is that good. It's just working off a bad brief.

Here's what happens when you fix it.

You spend two minutes running one prompt. The AI reads its own instructions back, tells you exactly what's dragging it down, and hands you a clean replacement. You paste that in. Done.

From that moment, it knows you. It knows how you like to work, what good output looks like to you, what to stop doing. The answers get sharper immediately. You stop repeating yourself. You stop fighting it. The tool you already pay for suddenly performs like the one you were promised.

This is the single highest-leverage thing you'll do to your AI all year, and it costs you two minutes. Everything else we do builds on top of it, so we start here.

WHERE TO RUN THIS

A brand-new chat, not inside a project. Copy your current instructions out of settings first, the prompt asks you to paste them in.

Here's how.

THE PROMPT · PASTE THIS IN
Audit my account custom instructions. Diagnosis first, then a suggested rewrite. Do not apply anything.

Everything you need is pasted below. Work only from that text. Do not ask me questions. Do not try to read my settings. If the block is empty, say so and stop.

Check for:
1. Length. Over 400 words? Name what's bloating it.
2. Rules that are vague, repeated, or contradict each other.
3. Reference material that shouldn't be here: pricing, client details, examples, research, anything that changes often. Say where it should move.
4. Anything missing that would improve everyday output.

Answer in this exact format. Nothing else.

WHAT'S WORKING: (max 3 bullets, one line each)
WHAT'S HURTING YOUR OUTPUT: (max 4 bullets, one line each, plainest language)
FIX THIS: (max 4 bullets. Each one: the problem, what to do, where it moves if it moves)
YOUR REWRITTEN VERSION: (the full replacement in a copy-paste block, 150 to 400 words. This is a draft for you to approve. I have not changed anything. Paste it in yourself only if you're happy.)

Keep every line short. Assume I'm time-poor. No preamble, no summary. Stop after the rewrite.
WHAT TO DO WITH THIS

Paste the rewrite into your custom instructions box and save. Account layer done. Everything else sits on top of it.

You'll know it worked when the rewrite is shorter than what you had, and you can't find anything in it you'd argue with.
BY HAND
  1. Copy the rewritten version.
  2. On ChatGPT: Settings, then Personalisation, then Custom Instructions. Replace what's there and save.
  3. On Claude: Settings, then Profile. Replace the preferences box contents and save.
  4. Old version worth keeping? Paste it into a note first. Then it's safe to overwrite.

If the box won't take the full draft, your plan has a smaller character limit. Ask it to cut the draft to 250 words and paste that instead. Free ChatGPT accounts cap the box at roughly 250 words; paid plans and Claude take the full version.

STEP 1C

There's a second thing shaping your answers, and it isn't your instructions.

  • It's also built its own memory of you. Some of it has gone stale.
  • One prompt lists what it remembers and flags what looks off.
  • Tell it to forget the stale bits, then confirm in settings.

Claude quietly builds up its own memory of you as you work, across all your chats.

Most of it is helpful. But some of it goes stale. An old job, a one-off you did months ago, a preference you've since changed. It's still in there, still nudging the answers, and you can't see it.

So even with clean instructions, the odd answer comes back slightly off, and you can't work out why.

This step shows you what it's holding onto, so you can clear anything that's out of date.

WHERE TO RUN THIS

A brand-new chat, not inside a project, with memory switched on in your settings.

Here's how.

THE PROMPT · PASTE THIS IN
Show me what you actually remember about me, so I can check it's right.

I don't mean my saved instructions. I mean the memory you've built up about me on your own over time, across our chats. List it back to me in plain language.

For each thing you remember, tell me:
- What you remember
- Whether it still looks current, or might be out of date

Then flag anything that looks stale, wrong, or too specific to one old job, because that could be quietly shaping your answers now.

Answer in this exact format. Nothing else.

WHAT I REMEMBER ABOUT YOU: (bullets, plain language, one line each)
MIGHT BE OUT OF DATE: (bullets, anything worth checking or clearing. If nothing, say so.)
HOW TO CLEAR IT: (one or two lines, plain steps, telling me where to remove or edit a memory myself.)

Keep it short and easy to understand. No jargon, no preamble. I'll clear anything myself. Change nothing.
WHAT TO DO WITH THIS

Flagged nothing? You're clean. Listed something stale? Tell it which entries to forget, or open memory settings and delete them by hand. Either way, check the settings screen afterwards. That list is the truth, a chat reply saying "done" isn't.

You'll know it worked when nothing in the list surprises you. Surprises are exactly what this step exists to catch.
BY HAND
  1. For anything stale, tell it in the chat: "Forget that I..." and name the entry.
  2. Then confirm it's actually gone. On ChatGPT: Settings, then Personalisation, then Manage Memories, bin anything still there.
  3. On Claude: Settings, then Capabilities, then Memory. View the list, edit or delete the stale lines.
  4. The settings list is the truth. If it's still on that list, it's still shaping your answers.

YOUR PROJECTS

Where the AI should be at its smartest. Do one project at a time, start with your messiest.

THE POINTA project done right turns a general assistant into a specialist in one job.
WHAT IT'S WORTHThe AI stops asking you the same questions and starts sounding like it's read your whole business.
WHAT MOST PEOPLE GET WRONGThey think more files makes a project smarter. A mess of files makes it dumber, because it treats all of them as equally true, including the out-of-date ones.
TIMETwenty to thirty minutes per project. One project properly beats five halfway.
WHICH ONE IS YOU? Project has instructions? Start at 2A. Files and history but the instructions box is empty? Start at 2A-i. Either way you'll end up at 2B, 2C and 2D.
STEP 2A

Your projects are where the AI should be at its smartest. Usually it's where they're at their most confused.

  • Projects rot fastest: old rules, dead files, clashing instructions.
  • The audit names what's hurting the project and rewrites its instructions.
  • Read, paste, done. The files come next.

A project is meant to be the AI's specialist mode. You set it up for one job, a client, an offer, a type of work, and load it with the instructions and files for that job. Done right, it stops being a general assistant and becomes an expert in that one thing. That's the whole point of them.

But projects rot faster than anything else in your setup. You build one, it works, you keep using it. Then the work shifts. You add a file, then another, then five more. You tweak the instructions mid-task and never clean up after. Six months on, the project's carrying instructions for work you don't do anymore, files that contradict each other, and rules that quietly fight the rules in your main account setup. The AI's trying to follow all of it at once, so it follows none of it well.

Here's the bit most people miss. Your project runs on your account instructions and the project's own instructions at the same time. Both are live at once. So if your account setup says one thing and your project says another, the AI's being pulled two ways, and the answer suffers for it. You feel it as the project going vague, or ignoring something you know you told it. It's not broken. It's trying to follow two briefs at once.

Here's what happens when you fix it.

You run one prompt inside the project. The AI reads its own instructions and files, and in plain English tells you what's outdated, what's duplicated, what's contradicting your account setup, and what should be moved out into a file instead of clogging the instructions. Then it hands you a clean rewrite.

You read it, you paste it in. The project goes back to being the specialist it was built to be. Sharper, faster, no more pulling in two directions.

Start with your messiest project, the one you know is bloated. One done properly beats five done halfway.

WHERE TO RUN THIS

Inside the project you're fixing. Open the project, start a new chat in there, and run it. Outside the project it can't see the instructions or files.

Here's how.

THE PROMPT · PASTE THIS IN
Audit this project's setup. Diagnosis first, then a suggested rewrite of the project instructions. Do not apply or change anything.

Read the files in this project yourself. For the project instructions, work from the block I've pasted at the bottom, because you can't reliably read those back on your own. Do not ask me questions. If the pasted block is empty, say so and stop.

Look at four things and explain each in plain, simple language:
1. Project instructions. What's unclear, out of date, repeated, or too vague to act on?
2. Clashes. Does anything here contradict my account-level instructions, or another rule inside this project? Name the clash so I can decide which wins.
3. Files. What's outdated, duplicated, or the wrong place for the information?
4. What's misplaced. What's crammed into the instructions that should really live in a file, because it's reference material or it changes often?

Answer in this exact format. Nothing else.

WHAT'S WORKING: (max 3 bullets, one line each)
WHAT'S HURTING THIS PROJECT: (max 5 bullets, one line each, plainest language. For any clash, say what fights what.)
FIX THIS: (max 5 bullets. Each one: the problem, what to do, where it moves if it moves)
YOUR REWRITTEN PROJECT INSTRUCTIONS: (the full replacement in a copy-paste block, as short as it can be while keeping everything that matters. This is a draft for you to approve. I have changed nothing. Paste it in yourself only if you're happy.)

Keep every line short and easy to understand. Assume I'm time-poor and not technical. No jargon, no preamble, no summary. Stop after the rewrite.

MY CURRENT PROJECT INSTRUCTIONS: [paste here]
WHAT TO DO WITH THIS

Don't paste yet. Read its file findings first, the next step plans those. Paste the rewritten project instructions in, then go to 2B for the files.

You'll know it worked when the audit names at least one thing you'd forgotten was even in there.
BY HAND
  1. Copy the rewritten project instructions.
  2. Open the project's instructions box: in ChatGPT it's in the project's settings, in Claude it's the instructions panel on the project page.
  3. Replace what's there and save.
  4. Leave the chat open. The file findings feed straight into 2B.
STEP 2A-i

Some projects grow up backwards. Files first, instructions never.

  • Files and history, but the instructions box is empty. It happens a lot.
  • The AI drafts the project's brief from what's already inside it.
  • Fix its assumptions, paste it in, then sort the files.

It happens more than you'd think. You start a project, you drag documents in as the work comes up, you have real conversations in it, and it works well enough that you never notice the instructions box is empty. Fifteen files deep, months of history, and the AI still has no standing brief for what this project is actually for. Every chat, it re-derives the job from scratch off whatever files it happens to weight that day. That's why the same project gives you a sharp answer on Tuesday and a vague one on Thursday.

You can't audit instructions that don't exist, so 2A has nothing to work with here. But the project isn't empty. Those files and that history are exactly the raw material good instructions get written from. So instead of you drafting a brief from a blank page, the AI reads what's already there and writes the first version for you.

Here's what happens when you run it.

The AI reads the files and the way you've been working in the project, works out what the project is for, and hands you draft instructions plus its list of assumptions so you can correct anything it got wrong. You read it, fix what's off, paste it in. Now the project has a spine, and you carry on to 2B to sort the files themselves.

WHERE TO RUN THIS

Inside the project. Open it, start a new chat in there, run the prompt.

Here's how.

THE PROMPT · PASTE THIS IN
This project has files and chat history but no project instructions. Draft them for me. Do not save or apply anything.

Everything you need is already here. Read the files in this project and how I've been using it. Do not ask me to paste anything. Do not send me a list of questions. Work out what this project is for from what's in front of you.

Keep the draft as short as it can be while covering: what this project is for, who or what it serves, how I want the work done here, the standards the output has to meet, and what to avoid. Do not copy reference material out of the files into the instructions. The files stay the source for facts. The instructions only say how to behave.

Answer in this exact format. Nothing else.

YOUR DRAFT PROJECT INSTRUCTIONS: (the full set in a copy-paste block. A draft for you to approve. I have saved nothing. Paste it in yourself only if you're happy.)

WHAT I ASSUMED: (max 4 bullets, one line each. The guesses you made about this project, so I can correct anything wrong before I use it.)

Keep it short. No preamble, no summary. Stop after the assumptions.
WHAT TO DO WITH THIS

Fix anything wrong in the assumptions, paste the instructions into the project's instructions box and save. Then go straight to 2B. Fifteen files that grew unsupervised will need the same sort-out as any other project, and now the AI has a brief to judge them against.

You'll know it worked when the draft describes the project the way you'd describe it to a new hire.
BY HAND
  1. Fix anything wrong in its assumptions first, in the chat.
  2. Copy the final draft.
  3. Open the project's instructions box, paste it in, save.
  4. Go to 2B in the same chat and sort the files.
STEP 2B

The files are what make a project actually smart. Most projects either have none, or a mess.

  • Instructions tell it how to behave. Files tell it what to know.
  • This step plans the file set: keep, bin, merge, and what's missing.
  • A map, not a build. You approve it before anything gets made.

Here's the difference between an AI that feels generic and one that feels like it's read your whole business. It's the files. Your instructions tell the AI how to behave. Your files tell it what to know. Pricing, your process, past decisions, client background, the stuff you'd otherwise re-type every time. Load that into files once and the AI carries it into every task in that project without being reminded.

Two ways this goes wrong. Either the project has no files at all, so the AI's working off memory and vibes and asking you the same questions forever. Or it's got files, but they've piled up, two versions of the same thing, notes that are a year out of date, one giant dumping-ground document nobody's opened since. Either way the AI can't tell what matters, so it treats all of it as equally true, including the bits that aren't anymore.

There's also a quieter trap. Loads of stuff that's crammed into your project instructions shouldn't be there at all, it should be a file. Reference material, examples, anything that changes. When it sits in the instructions it bloats them and drags every answer down. Moving it into a file fixes two problems at once: the instructions get lighter, and the knowledge gets a proper home.

Here's what happens when you fix it.

You run one prompt. The AI looks at the project and works out what files it should have. If files already exist, it tells you which to keep, which to bin, which to merge, and what's missing. If there are none, it designs the set from scratch. And if the project genuinely doesn't need files, it'll tell you that too, and why. Either way you get a simple plan: what each file is for, what goes in it, what stays out.

It doesn't build anything yet. This is the map. You approve it, then we create the files one at a time, properly, in the next step.

WHERE TO RUN THIS

Inside the same project, ideally the same chat you ran 2A in, so it still has the audit in front of it.

Here's how.

THE PROMPT · PASTE THIS IN
Plan the knowledge files this project should have. Recommend only. Do not create, write, or change any files.

Read the existing files in this project yourself. For the project instructions, work from the block I've pasted at the bottom, because you can't reliably read those back on your own. Do not ask me questions first. Work from what's in front of you.

First, check what already exists:
- If the project has files, audit them: what's outdated, what's duplicated, what's in the wrong place, what's missing.
- If the project has no files, design the set it should have from scratch.
- If the project genuinely doesn't need files, say so and explain why. Don't invent files to look useful.
- Either way, flag anything currently stuffed in the project instructions that should be pulled out into a file instead.

Then give me the plan. For each file you recommend, in plain simple language:
- File name
- What it's for, in one line
- What goes in it
- What stays out of it
- Is it fixed reference, or something that'll change over time
- If it replaces or absorbs something that already exists, say what

Answer in this exact format. Nothing else.

WHAT YOU'VE GOT NOW: (max 4 bullets. Current file situation. If none, say so.)
THE FILES THIS PROJECT SHOULD HAVE: (the recommended set, each laid out as above. If none needed, say so and why.)
WHAT TO MOVE OUT OF INSTRUCTIONS: (max 4 bullets. Anything in the instructions that belongs in a file instead. If nothing, say so.)

Keep the set as small as it can be. Do not invent files to look thorough. Do not duplicate the same information across two files. Keep every line short and easy to understand, assume I'm time-poor and not technical. No jargon, no preamble. Stop after the plan. Do not create anything.

MY CURRENT PROJECT INSTRUCTIONS: [paste here]
WHAT TO DO WITH THIS

A plan, not a build. Move-out-of-instructions items wait for your next instructions update. Old files to merge or bin are your call by hand. New files you build one at a time, next step. Pick the most important and go to 2C.

You'll know it worked when the plan is smaller than you expected. A short list of files with clear jobs beats a long one.
BY HAND
  1. Nothing gets built yet. This is the map.
  2. Files it said to bin: open the project's file list and remove them yourself. Unsure about one? Download it first, then remove it.
  3. Files it said to merge: remove the old ones once the merged version exists in 2C, not before.
  4. Pick the most important new file on the plan and take it to 2C.
STEP 2C

Now you build the files. One at a time, the right way, or you'll create a fresh mess.

  • Build one file at a time. Never let it build everything at once.
  • Anything it isn't sure of gets marked NEEDS CONFIRMING, not guessed.
  • Check it, attach it, move to the next.

You've got the plan. This is where it becomes real. But there's a trap right at the start, and it's the reason most people's projects end up cluttered again three months later: they let the AI build everything at once, and it invents half of it.

So two rules here, and they matter. One file at a time. And you tell the AI to use only what you've actually given it, and to flag anything it's unsure of instead of filling the gap with a guess. Left to its own devices it will sometimes invent a detail to be helpful. This stops that. A file full of confident-sounding guesses is worse than no file.

There's a second thing most people get wrong: they treat every piece of knowledge the same way. It isn't. Some knowledge should be a written document the AI reads. Some should be a live link to a Google Doc or Sheet you already keep updated, so it never goes stale. And some is better as a small structured reference, a table or checklist, where the format is the point. Put a thing in the wrong home and it either rots, or it's useless when you need it.

So before it writes a single word, the AI tells you which of the three this file should be, and why. You'll almost always know instantly whether it's right.

Here's what happens when you do this properly.

You point the AI at one file from your plan. It picks the right format, builds just that one, uses only what it actually knows, and marks anything it's unsure about so you can fill it in. You check it, you're happy, you move to the next. Slow is the point. Five clean files beat fifteen half-invented ones.

Inside the project, same chat as 2B if you still have it open, so the plan is in front of it.

Here's how.

THE PROMPT · PASTE THIS IN
Build one of the knowledge files you recommended. Just one. Do not build any others.

Build this one: [NAME THE FILE FROM YOUR PLAN, e.g. "Objection handling"]

You recommended this file and the format for it in the plan. If that plan is still in this chat, follow it. If the plan's gone, tell me in one line what you'd recommend this file be and the format for it, then build it.

Rules:
- Use only what's in this project or what I've told you. Do not invent details, names or numbers to fill gaps.
- Anything you're not sure of, mark [NEEDS CONFIRMING] and leave it for me. Don't guess.
- Keep it clean and easy to update later.

Answer in this format. Nothing else.

FORMAT: (written document, live link, or structured reference, and one line on why. If a live link is right, don't copy content in, tell me what to link.)
THE FILE: (finished, in a copy-paste block)
STILL NEEDS CONFIRMING: (bullets, or "nothing, this one's done")
